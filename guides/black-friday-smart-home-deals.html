Black Friday smart home device deals: what to check before you buy

Updated November 15, 2026

A smart home deal can look like a deep cut and still be last year's model, a hub the box leaves out, or a camera whose saved video costs a yearly fee the sale price skips. The standard printed on the box, what the install needs, and this model's own prices tell you whether the discount is on the device you meant to buy.

Older-generation and clearance models

The family name is a group. The model in the cart is the device. A speaker, a display, a plug, a bulb, a doorbell, a camera, a thermostat, or a lock from last year is a different product from the one a review of the new name describes. A clearance listing can be that older model, or a sale-only variant that drops the hub, the display, or a sensor. Write the full name from the cart, including any number, and match it to the sheet.

A very low price on a model several generations back can be stock that no longer receives firmware updates. That is a smaller offer: a device that stays in the home after the fixes stop. An older model that still receives updates, and that works with the devices you already have, can be the better buy. Judge each model against its own prices. A chart for the current model does not answer for the clearance one.

The voice assistant you already use

Read which voice assistant the sheet says the device works with, and compare that with the voice assistant you already use. A device that answers a different assistant is a device you will talk to a different way, or not at all. A badge that only says it works with voice has not named which assistant. If the sheet names none, pause.

Read the smart-home standard printed on the box. The same family name can ship as two products: one that uses the standard your other devices use, and one that does not. A plug, a bulb, a lock, or a sensor on a different standard will not join the ones you have. Two standards on one sheet can still mean a hub is required for one of them. The next section is that check. If the sheet names no standard and no assistant, you do not yet know what the device will join.

A hub, and local control versus the cloud

Some devices join the Wi-Fi you already have and need nothing else in the box. Some need a hub or a bridge, and the sale photo can show one the cart does not include. Read the list of what is in the box. A hub sold separately is a second purchase. Add its price when you do not already own a compatible one. A bridge that only connects one group of devices is not a hub for a different group. Match the line on this sheet. Whether the router or the mesh system is the right one for the house is a different check, in Black Friday Wi-Fi router and mesh system deals. This page stays with the device.

Local control means a switch, a schedule, or a scene still runs when the outside connection does not. Cloud-only means the device waits for an account before it will do the thing you bought it for. Read which features the sheet says work from a button on the device, with no account, and which exist only in the app. A plug you can still switch by hand is a different product from a plug that does nothing until the app answers. If the page is silent, that is an unknown.

Speakers and displays

A smart speaker plays audio and listens for the voice assistant the sheet names. A smart display adds a screen. The two can share a family name and differ in the microphones, the camera, and whether a hub is built in. Match the model in the cart. A display that only works as a screen for one assistant is not a speaker you can move to another. Read what the sheet says still works from the buttons on the device if you decline the account.

Read what the microphones and the camera do, and how you mute them. A speaker in a bedroom and a display by the door are different privacy choices. Those settings are in the privacy section below. The sale price is the device. If the sheet requires a music or video service, that fee belongs in the total for as long as you will keep it. A service you will not use stays out.

Plugs, bulbs, and the per-unit price

A smart plug sits between a lamp or a small appliance and the outlet. A smart bulb replaces a bulb. Read the form on the sheet: the plug's outlet shape, and the bulb's base, shape, and whether it dims. A bulb that does not fit the lamp, or a plug that does not fit the outlet, is a different product from the one in the photo. A color bulb and a white bulb can share a family name. Match the one in the cart. A space heater is a different load from a lamp. It plugs into a wall outlet on its own. Coverage, wattage, and filter cost for a purifier, a heater, or a humidifier are in Black Friday air purifier, space heater and humidifier deals.

Packs are different products. A 2-pack and a 4-pack do not share a low. Divide the checkout price by the number of devices in the cart. Compare that per-unit price with one of the same model sold alone. A pack that mixes a newer device with an older one is not one price. Count only the units you will install. A spare you will not use is not part of the saving. A starter kit that bundles a hub with the bulbs or the plugs is a package. Pricing the pieces you would buy, and ignoring one you would not, is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding?

Doorbells, cameras, and the yearly fee

A video doorbell watches the door. A security camera watches a room, a porch, or a yard. Read whether this model records, and where the recording stays: on a card in the device, on a hub in the home, or in an account. A live view with no saved video is a different product from one that keeps a history. Person detection, a package alert, and professional monitoring can each sit behind a subscription. Professional monitoring means a person is supposed to answer an alert. Read who the sheet says answers, and what still works if you stop paying. The camera can remain a camera when that fee stops, or it can lose the alert. The sheet says which.

The sale price is the device. Add the monthly or yearly fee for every period you will keep video history, person detection, or monitoring. A trial that renews costs money unless you cancel on the printed terms. Features you will not use stay out of the total. A card the camera accepts, if the sheet says so, is a different cost from a cloud history fee, and a card the cart leaves out is a second purchase. If the sheet ties any other feature to a fee, add that fee the same way, for as long as you will keep it. A camera that records from the windshield is a different product. Sensor resolution, parking mode, and a hardwire kit are in Black Friday dash cam and car tech deals.

Thermostats, locks, and the install

A smart thermostat replaces the one on the wall. Read whether this model needs a C-wire, the common wire that supplies steady power, whether it includes an adapter, or whether it runs on batteries. If the wall has no C-wire and the sheet requires one, the discount is on a thermostat you cannot install as the photo shows. An adapter sold separately is a second purchase. The letters on the wires you already have are the fact. A photo of a different home's wiring answers for that other home. Turn the power off before you remove a wired thermostat, and stop if the wires do not match the sheet.

A smart lock replaces the deadbolt or the latch, or it fits over the lock you have. Read which the sheet describes. Measure the door's thickness and the backset, the distance from the edge of the door to the center of the hole, and compare both with the range on the sheet. A lock that needs a new hole is a different install from one that uses the holes already in the door. Handing, if the sheet names it, is which way the door swings. A missing line is an unknown. Measure before you pay. Taking the lock off to send it back can count as opened. That window is in Black Friday return policies.

A wired doorbell uses the doorbell wiring and the transformer you already have, or ones the sheet says you need. If the sheet lists a voltage, compare it with the transformer at the door. If it lists none, that requirement is an unknown. Read whether the sheet says the doorbell works with the chime you have, and whether a connector is in the box. Turn the power off before you remove a wired doorbell. A battery doorbell skips those wires. The trade-off is in the next section. A camera that needs a mount, a power adapter, or a base station is missing those when the cart leaves them out. Price the pieces that are actually in the box.

Battery versus wired

A battery device goes up without new wiring and comes down to charge, or to replace the cells the sheet names. A wired device stays powered and needs the wiring, the transformer, or the adapter the sheet describes. Some doorbells, locks, and cameras are wired and keep a battery for a power cut. Read which this model is. A photo of a wire does not mean the cart includes a wired unit. A plug and a bulb take power from the socket. Their ongoing cost is the pack price and any hub, not a cell you swap.

Add the cells or the packs you will keep buying, for as long as you will keep the device, to the checkout price. If the listing does not price them, that cost is an unknown. A battery that is built in and not replaceable is a different offer from one you can swap. The interval on the sheet is a claim. Weather, how often a camera wakes, and the cells you use can change it. The return window is how you learn whether that claim holds at your door. What counts as opened, plus a restocking fee and a final-sale label, is in Black Friday return policies.

Privacy settings and firmware updates

A speaker listens. A display and a camera can watch. A doorbell can see the street, a neighbor's door, or a shared hallway. Read what the sheet says is stored, whether it stays on the device or goes to an account, and how you delete it, including before a return. Read how you mute the microphone and how you cover or disable the camera. If the camera sees a shared space, read any notice the sheet describes before you rely on the recording. If the page is silent, you do not yet know. Do not take the answer from another model in the family.

Firmware that fixes security problems is part of the purchase, because the device sits in the home. Read how long this model receives updates, and the date of the latest firmware if the maker publishes one. If the support page no longer lists the model, further updates are unknown. A clearance price on a model past that window is a device you would keep after the fixes stop. An older model that is still on the support page can be the better buy. When to wait

Wait if the model, the voice assistant, the standard on the box, the hub, or the install requirement is missing. Wait if a subscription you will keep is left out of the total, if firmware updates are unclear, or if the door, the doorbell transformer, or the thermostat wiring does not match the sheet. Wait if checkout is lower only next to a reference price. Compare Friday and Monday in Black Friday vs Cyber Monday, and an early label in early Black Friday deals. Buy when the cart matches the sheet and checkout is at or under this model's recent low, after the hub and the fees you will keep. A delivery date is in holiday shipping cutoffs.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the sheet, and this model's prices.

  1. 1Is the model in the cart the one I mean, and does it still receive firmware updates?
  2. 2Does the sheet name the voice assistant I already use, and the smart-home standard printed on the box?
  3. 3Does this device need a hub or a bridge, and is that hub in the cart or already in the home?
  4. 4Which features work from the device with no account, and which exist only in the cloud?
  5. 5If the sheet lists video history, person detection, or monitoring, what do those features cost for the years I will keep them?
  6. 6On a multipack, is the per-unit price lower than the same device sold alone, counting only units I will install?
  7. 7If this is a doorbell, a thermostat, or a lock, does the wiring or the door match the sheet, and is the device battery or wired?
  8. 8What does the return window say once it is installed, and is checkout at or under this model's recent low after the hub and the fees?

When those match, the deal is this device.

Questions people ask

Is an older smart speaker, camera, or lock still a good deal?

An older model is a sound buy when it still receives firmware updates and it works with the voice assistant you already use and the smart-home standard printed on the box. A newer model does not make an older one join a standard the sheet does not list. Read this model's own recent prices. Another generation has its own chart.

How do I tell whether it works with the devices I already have?

Read the voice assistant the sheet names and compare it with the voice assistant you already use. Read the smart-home standard printed on the box and compare it with the devices you already have. If the sheet says a hub or a bridge is required, that hub has to be in the cart or already in the home. A badge that only says compatible has not named either one.

How do I count a video-history or monitoring subscription?

Add the fee for every period you will keep video history, person detection, or professional monitoring. A renewing trial costs money unless you cancel on the printed terms. Features you will not use stay out of the total. The sale price is the device.

Is a multi-pack cheaper than the same device sold alone?

Divide the pack's checkout price by the number of devices in the cart, and compare that with one of the same model sold alone. Count only the units you will install. A 2-pack and a 4-pack do not share a price chart.

What should I measure before a doorbell, a thermostat, or a lock?

For a doorbell, compare the transformer's voltage with the voltage the sheet requires, if the sheet lists one. For a thermostat, read whether it needs a C-wire or includes an adapter. For a lock, measure the door's thickness and the backset, the distance from the edge of the door to the center of the hole, and compare both with the sheet. A missing line is an unknown.

What happens to the return window after I install it?

Installing a lock, a thermostat, or a doorbell can count as opened. Read whether this offer accepts an installed item, charges a restocking fee, or marks it final sale. The policy linked from this cart is the one that applies.
