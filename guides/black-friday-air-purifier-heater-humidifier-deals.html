Room size, coverage, and air changes

A square-foot coverage line assumes a ceiling height and how many times an hour the room's air should pass through the filter. That count is the air changes. A bigger room or a higher ceiling means fewer of them from the same unit. Multiply length by width by height, in feet. If the sheet omits the ceiling or the target, the badge does not name your room. A number from the highest fan speed is not the quieter speed you will leave on.

What CADR measures

CADR is filtered air per minute, often in cubic feet, often split into smoke, dust, and pollen. Read the smoke figure for the smallest of those. No particle name, or no CADR, and the square-foot line is incomplete. One common claim multiplies CADR by 1.55 to get square feet. That multiplier assumes an 8-foot ceiling and 4.8 air changes an hour. It is not a measurement of your room. Your air changes equal CADR times 60, divided by your cubic feet, at the speed you will run.

HEPA, and "HEPA-type"

In the common U.S. definition, HEPA means a filter tested to capture 99.97 percent of particles 0.3 micrometers across. Read both numbers here. "HEPA-type," "HEPA-like," and "HEPA-style" do not name that test. Air has to go through the filter. A gap around the frame skips it, and you see that during the return window, not in the photo.

Replacement filters, and what they cost

Yearly filter cost = (price of one replacement set) × 12 ÷ (months between replacements on this sheet). Add the purchase price to that yearly cost times the years you keep the unit. A missing interval or price leaves the total unknown. Do not borrow another model's interval. The filter has to be sold for this model number. A version sold by one seller can use a filter only that seller stocks, and a cheap unit with a costly or missing filter can cost more later.

Noise on the speed you will use

Read the decibel figure for the speed you will leave on. A low-speed number in the headline is often the speed that misses the CADR you counted. One figure with no speed does not name the setting. "Quiet" is not a measurement. The return window is when you hear that setting in your room.

Ionizers and ozone

An ionizer, a plasma feature, or an ozone setting is a different function from the filter. Ozone is a lung irritant. Read whether this sheet lists one, and whether the unit meets the ozone limit where you live. Some places restrict air cleaners that emit ozone. Silence on this model means that question is unknown. Do not copy another model's answer.

A different model number

Write the full model number, suffix included. One changed character can mean a different filter, sensor, CADR, or warranty, while the family name still matches a review. A version sold by one seller has its own sheet, and another number's price chart is a different unit. The same kind of suffix on an air fryer, a blender, or a coffee maker is in Black Friday kitchen appliance deals.

Heater wattage and the room

Wattage is the draw while the element is on. A common rule of thumb is about 10 watts per square foot of a typical room. Drafts, a high ceiling, or weak insulation need more, and a small closed room can need less. A portable heater on a standard household outlet is commonly rated up to about 1,500 watts, near that outlet's limit, which is not a measurement of your room. No wattage on the sheet means you do not have the draw.

Tip-over, overheat, and a certification mark

Look for a tip-over switch, an overheat shutoff, and a certification mark for this model, such as UL, ETL, or CSA. A missing line is unknown, and a mark on another model does not transfer. Keep the clearance the sheet states, off bedding, curtains, and upholstery. Plug straight into a wall outlet. An extension cord or a power strip can overheat under that draw. If the outlet is far, move the heater. A smart plug does not fix that distance. What a plug or a thermostat is for is in Black Friday smart home device deals.

Ceramic, oil-filled, and infrared

A ceramic heater fans air over an element. The room's air warms while the fan runs and starts to cool when the element cycles off. You hear the fan. An oil-filled heater warms a sealed reservoir, with no fan, more slowly, and keeps giving off heat after the element cycles off. You do not add oil. An infrared heater, also called radiant, warms people and objects in its path more than the air across the room. The sheet should say which of the three this is.

What a heater costs to run

Kilowatt-hours = (watts ÷ 1,000) × hours the element is actually on. Cost = kilowatt-hours × your price per kilowatt-hour. A 1,500-watt element on for one hour uses 1.5 kilowatt-hours, so that hour costs 1.5 times your rate. A thermostat means the element is not on every plugged-in hour, so the nameplate is the draw while it heats. Without your rate, compare the kilowatt-hours. You cannot finish a dollar figure. The sensor sits at the heater, warmer than the far side of the room, so a setpoint is not the temperature where you sit. No accuracy on the sheet means you do not have one. No thermostat means it runs until you switch it off.

Evaporative, ultrasonic, and warm mist

An evaporative humidifier blows air through a wet wick. Output falls as the room's humidity rises, and minerals tend to stay in the wick you replace. An ultrasonic humidifier mists the water itself. It is usually quieter, and minerals leave as dust unless you use distilled water or a demineralization cartridge the sheet names. A warm-mist unit heats the water, draws more power, and blows hot vapor, so keep the outlet away from children and pets. Put this sheet's wattage into the running-cost formula. A germ claim needs the test printed on this sheet.

White dust and cleaning

White dust is mineral from an ultrasonic mist. A wick holds most of it. Warm mist tends to leave minerals in the tank as scale instead. Empty, rinse, and dry every tank on the schedule the sheet gives. A tank you cannot open fully is harder to dry, and a film grows when it stays wet. No cleaning steps means that work is unknown.

Tank size, refills, and wick cost

Tank size is water held, not humidity added. More volume means fewer refills. Listed hours belong to a stated setting, and volume alone is not hours. When the sheet gives usable water and the rate it is used, runtime is the water divided by that rate. Yearly wick or cartridge cost = (the part's price) × (12 ÷ months between replacements). Add that for the years you keep the unit. The part has to be sold for this model number.

Dehumidifier capacity

Capacity is water removed in 24 hours, in pints or liters, at a stated temperature and humidity. The condition is part of the number. A cooler room can remove less, and a rating with no condition cannot be compared. A room-size line on the box assumes that test. A tank you empty and a hose to a drain are different offers. No hose fitting means you empty the tank. When the sheet lists pints per kilowatt-hour, energy for a rated day = daily pints ÷ that figure, and the cost is that energy times your rate.

A "was," "list," or "compare at" figure is a reference the seller chose. It may never have sold, or it may belong to a filter pack this box does not include. A unit sold with extra filters

Subtract the unit-only price, same model and condition. The remainder is the filters. Compare it with those filters sold alone for this model. A filter you would not buy counts as nothing. A "free" filter counts only when it fits this model and you would have bought it. The rest of the split is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding?

Sending a bulky box back

These cartons are bulky, and a dehumidifier is the heavy one. Read the restocking fee, who pays shipping, and whether opening the unit or the filter bag counts as opened. That window is also how you learn the noise, the dust, and whether the thermostat holds the room. Windows and final sale are in Black Friday return policies. The warranty

The motor, the fan, the heating element, and the housing can be different lengths. Filters, wicks, and cartridges are consumables, so a warranty that skips them has not paid the yearly cost above. Wait when CADR, the HEPA wording, or a filter for this model is missing. Wait when a heater lacks a tip-over switch, an overheat shutoff, or a certification mark, or the only way to plug it in is an extension cord. Answer these from the cart, the sheet, the return policy, and this model's prices.

Questions people ask

How do I match an air purifier to my room?

Measure length times width times height, in feet. Air changes per hour equal the CADR of the speed you will run, times 60, divided by that volume. A square-foot line assumes a ceiling and an air-change target. If the sheet omits both, you do not know which room it describes.

Does HEPA-type mean the same thing as HEPA?

HEPA-type, HEPA-like, and HEPA-style do not name the HEPA test. In the common U.S. definition, HEPA means a filter tested to capture 99.97 percent of particles 0.3 micrometers across. Read the capture rate and the particle size on this sheet.

How do I estimate what a space heater costs to run?

Kilowatt-hours equal watts divided by 1,000, times hours the element is actually on. Cost equals those kilowatt-hours times your price per kilowatt-hour. A 1,500-watt element on for one hour uses 1.5 kilowatt-hours, so that hour costs 1.5 times your rate. A thermostat means it is not on every plugged-in hour.

Which safety lines should a space heater list?

A tip-over switch, an overheat shutoff, and a certification mark for this model, such as UL, ETL, or CSA. Plug it into a wall outlet, not an extension cord or a power strip. A missing line is unknown.

Why does a humidifier leave white dust?

An ultrasonic unit mists minerals out with the water, and they settle as dust. A wick holds most of them. Warm mist tends to leave them as scale in the tank. Distilled water or a cartridge the sheet names cuts the dust. You still empty and dry the tank.
