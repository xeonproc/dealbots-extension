Black Friday mattress deals: what to check before you buy

Updated November 8, 2026

A mattress deal can look like a deep cut and still be a different construction, a different size, or the same model at a price it has already been selling for. The build, the lines on this model's sheet, and this model's own prices tell you whether the discount is on the mattress you meant to buy.

Construction is the product

A percent off does not make two mattresses the same product. The construction is the way the mattress holds you up. The four names that show up on mattress sheets are innerspring, memory foam, latex, and hybrid. A sheet that does not name one has not told you which you are buying. A review of a different construction, even at the same size and the same percent off, describes that other mattress.

Innerspring

An innerspring uses a core of steel coils, with a comfort layer on top when the sheet lists one. You press down, and the surface pushes back. Air can move through an open coil core more easily than through a solid block of foam. If the sheet lists a coil count or a wire gauge, that line is about this model. Compare that count with another innerspring. The count does not, by itself, say how you will sleep.

Memory foam

Memory foam softens where your body warms and presses it, and it is slower to spring back. The surface contours. A solid foam core can hold heat closer to you than an open coil core. Whether this model sleeps warm is a line on the sheet, if the sheet mentions airflow, a cover, or a gel. If that line is missing, you do not yet know. If the sheet lists a foam density, that number is about this model. A missing density is an unknown. Do not take it from another model in the series.

Latex

Latex is a foam made from rubber. It usually springs back faster than memory foam, so it feels less like a slow sink and more like a buoyant surface. If the sheet says the latex is natural, synthetic, or a blend, that is the latex you are buying. A sheet that only says latex has not told you which. Two latex mattresses can still differ in thickness and firmness. The percent off does not close that gap.

Hybrid

A hybrid puts coils under a comfort layer of foam, latex, or both. It is its own construction. Read the layers this sheet lists. A thin quilted top over coils is an innerspring. A mattress with no coils is foam or latex. The word hybrid on the badge is a label until those layers are named.

A pillow-top is a layer sewn onto the cover. It is a cover style, not a construction. An innerspring pillow-top and a memory-foam pillow-top are still those two builds. Match the construction first, then compare prices among mattresses of that construction. A cheaper innerspring and a cheaper latex mattress are different mattresses.

What the sheet lists

Open the sheet for the model in the cart. If you cannot find one, pause. You are being asked to buy a mattress you cannot compare. Read the lines below for this model. A missing line is an unknown. It is not a match for another model in the same series.

Thickness

Thickness is the height, printed in inches. A thicker mattress in the same series is a different product from a thinner one. It can change how high you sit in bed, and whether the sheets you already own will fit. A sale version can be the thinner one under a familiar series name. Read the number on this model. A number you remember from a review of the series does not transfer.

Firmness

Firmness words, such as plush, medium, and firm, are the seller's labels. Two sheets can use the same word for different feels. If the sheet prints a number on a scale, read whose scale it is. The word on this sheet describes this model. It does not line up with the same word on another seller's sheet unless both say they use the same scale. A side sleeper and a back sleeper can want different points on that label. The sheet will not choose for you. It will tell you which label this model carries.

Edge support

Edge support is whether the side holds you when you sit on it to get up, or when you sleep near the edge. If the sheet lists a reinforced edge, a firmer perimeter, or coils that run to the border, that is the claim. If the line is missing, you do not yet know. A review of another model, even one with the same series name, does not fill it in.

Size

Twin, twin XL, full, queen, king, and California king are different mattresses. A split king is two narrower mattresses sold as a pair. The usual U.S. sizes are a twin at 38 by 75 inches, a twin XL at 38 by 80, a full at 54 by 75, a queen at 60 by 80, a king at 76 by 80, and a California king at 72 by 84. A sheet that prints different inches is selling those inches. Measure the bed, then match the size on the cart to that measurement.

The size in the headline and the size in the cart can differ. A page that says queen while the cart line says king is the king, at the king's price. A price chart for the queen answers for the queen. Read the size on the cart before you compare a price. If the listing is a mattress plus pillows, sheets, or a foundation priced as one package, price the pieces you would actually keep. If the sheet names a foam certification or a flammability standard, write that name down as a line on this model. A missing certification is an unknown. It does not rename the mattress, and it does not fill in a thickness, a firmness, or a size the sheet left blank.

Compare-at prices and this model's history

The number next to "was," "list," "compare at," or a percent off is a reference the seller chose. On a mattress, that number is often a price this model has not actually been selling at. The badge math can be tidy and still describe the ordinary price. The dollar price at checkout is the fact. This model's own recent selling prices are the other fact. How a reference price inflates the percent, including a short markup just before a sale, is in how to tell if a sale is real.

This model's recent selling prices

Read the chart for this exact model, in this size, in this condition. A low for the queen, for a different firmness, or for a similar series name is a low for that other mattress. New, floor-model, open-box, and refurbished do not share a chart. About 30 to 90 days, when the chart has them. Thirty days shows a short markup just before the sale. Ninety days is the safer window when that model has already been on sale more than once. The typical price is where offers cluster. The recent low is the bottom. Those shapes are in how to read a price history chart.

A sale-only model can have a short chart, or none, because it has not been sold for long. On a short chart, the first advertised price and the sale price can both sit inside the same brief window. Read the dates. If the "was" price only appears for a few days before the sale, or never appears as a selling price, the percent off is a label. You can set today's checkout next to a similar model's recent low and decide whether the construction and the sheet are worth the gap. That does not turn the other model's low into this model's low.

Series name and the model in the cart

The series name is a family. The model in the cart is the mattress. A sale can offer a near-identical variant, sometimes only then. One change can mark a different thickness, a different firmness, a different cover, a shorter warranty, or a size the headline did not lead with. The photo can match the mattress people reviewed. The line in the cart is the mattress you would receive.

A white-label twin is a build sold under more than one name. The names can look unrelated, and one of them may exist for a single seller or for the sale. Near-identical means the photo and the series name are close, while the cart carries a different sheet. Treat two names as the same mattress only when the sheets match. Until then they are two products, and they do not share a price. A chart for the other name answers for that other name.

Write the full name from the cart, including any number, size, and firmness. Match it to the spec sheet and to the price chart before you compare a price. A review of the series describes the model that review named. If the cart shows a shorter name than the sheet, stop and find the full one. If you cannot find a sheet for the exact model, pause. You are being asked to buy a mattress you cannot compare.

The same kind of mismatch is why a Black Friday TV model number may not match the set in a review, and why a kitchen appliance can be a seller-exclusive model under a familiar family name. On a mattress the differences sit in the construction, the thickness, the firmness, and the size. The warranty length on this model's sheet is the coverage you are buying. A length you remember from the reviewed model does not transfer.

Sleep trial, fees, and final sale

A sleep trial, if the seller offers one, is time to sleep on the mattress and still send it back. It is a different clock from the ordinary return window. The trial can list its own count of nights. It can start at delivery, or after a period the policy names. It can allow a refund, or only an exchange. It can charge a restocking fee, or a fee to pick the mattress up, that the ordinary window does not. Read those lines on this offer. A length you remember from another mattress does not transfer.

Pickup of the mattress you already own is a separate line from pickup of this one if you send it back. If the offer includes haul-away of the old mattress, read whether that pickup is in the checkout price or a fee added later, and whether someone has to be home. If the page does not offer it, moving the old mattress is your errand. A fee you will pay belongs in the price you compare.

A final-sale label means this offer is not a trial and not an ordinary return. The label can be on the product page, in the cart, or in the policy, including on a floor model or a sale-only variant. If the listing says final sale, you are keeping it. The general rules for a holiday window, a restocking fee, an opened item, and a final-sale label are in Black Friday return policies. This page stays with the trial a mattress listing may add. The policy linked from this cart is the one that applies. If the page does not state a trial or a window, find the policy before you pay. A missing line is an unknown.

Foundation, warranty, and a protection plan

Some warranties require the base under the mattress. If the sheet or the warranty lists a foundation, a box spring, a solid platform, an adjustable base, or slats no farther apart than a gap it names, a base outside that line can void the warranty. The requirement on this model is the one that counts. A base that worked for a different mattress does not answer it. If the sheet is silent, you do not yet know whether your bed frame is inside the warranty.

A foundation the warranty requires, and that you do not already own, is part of the price. Add it before you call the percent off real. A box spring, a slatted frame, and an adjustable base are different supports. Read which ones this warranty allows. The fee to haul the old mattress away, if you will pay it, sits in that same total.

A protection plan offered at checkout is a second purchase. It does not replace a foundation the warranty requires, and it does not turn a sale-only model into the one a review covered. Compare the plan with the warranty this model already includes. How to price that plan is in extended warranties and protection plans.

Delivery by a date

If the mattress has to arrive by a date, the delivery window is part of the decision. A low price that shows up after that date is a different purchase from the same price in the room. Read the date on this offer. A mattress can move on a different schedule from a small parcel on the same site. The date printed for this mattress is the one you have.

If the page offers in-home delivery or a drop at the door, those can be different dates and different fees. Read whether someone has to be home, and whether a delivery fee is still outside the percent off. Add that fee to checkout before you compare the total with this model's recent prices. Floor model, open-box, and refurbished

A floor model is a mattress that has been on display for people to try. Open-box can mean a return, including a return during a sleep trial, that is being sold again. Refurbished can mean the seller inspected it, cleaned it, or replaced a cover. Those words are different conditions, and none of them means new. The word on this listing is the condition you are buying.

Match the condition to the chart. A low for the new mattress is a low for the new one. A floor model can be the same construction at a lower checkout price, with a shorter trial, a different warranty, or no trial at all. Read those lines on this offer. A window you remember from the new version does not transfer. How refurbished, open-box, and new compare is in refurbished vs new on Black Friday.

When to wait

Wait when the sheet cannot answer the checks yet. A missing construction, a missing size, or a model you cannot find a sheet for is a reason to stop. The percent off can stay on the page while you look those up. It is not evidence that the price is low.

Wait when today's checkout sits near the typical price, or only under a reference price this model has not been selling at. A holiday label does not make that a new low. The named Friday and the named Monday are not automatically the cheaper day for this mattress. Compare the two against this model's own prices, which is the point of Black Friday vs Cyber Monday. An early label, weeks before the weekend, is the same comparison, covered in early Black Friday deals.

Wait, or at least keep the claim in its place, when the cart is a sale-only or white-label variant with a short chart or none. You can buy it when the sheet matches what you need and you accept the price next to a similar model's recent low. The badge is not what makes that decision. A chart you do not have yet is a reason the percent off is still a label.

You do not have to wait when the model in the cart matches the sheet and the chart, the construction, thickness, firmness, and size are the ones you will sleep on, and checkout is at or under this model's own recent low. A later label that only ties that price is the same price with a different headline. If the mattress has to arrive by a date, the delivery date can matter more than another dip. A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the spec sheet, this model's prices, and the trial terms on this offer.

  1. 1Is this an innerspring, memory foam, latex, or hybrid, and am I comparing it with that same construction?
  2. 2Do the thickness, firmness, and edge support on this sheet match how I will sleep on it?
  3. 3Does the size in the cart match the bed I measured?
  4. 4Is today's checkout price at or under this model's own recent selling prices, not only under a compare-at price?
  5. 5Is the model in the cart the one I mean, or a near-identical, white-label, or sale-only variant?
  6. 6If there is a sleep trial, what does sending it back cost, and is this offer final sale?
  7. 7Does the warranty require a foundation or box spring I have, separate from any protection plan at checkout?
  8. 8Will it arrive by the date I need, and is this new, or a floor model, open-box, or refurbished unit?

When the model matches, the construction and the size are the ones you will sleep on, and checkout is at or under this model's own recent low, the deal is about this mattress. If the chart belongs to another size or another name, skip the percent off until you have this model's price.

Questions people ask

Does a percent off make two mattresses the same?

An innerspring, a memory foam mattress, a latex mattress, and a hybrid are different products. A percent off uses a reference price the seller chose. It does not make two constructions the same mattress. Compare prices among mattresses of the construction you want, then read this model's own recent selling prices.

What should I read on the mattress spec sheet?

Read the thickness, the firmness label, whether edge support is listed, and the size, including whether the size in the cart matches the one you measured. Twin, full, queen, and king are different mattresses. If the sheet names a foam certification or a flammability standard, that line is about this model. A missing line is an unknown.

Is the was price a price this mattress has sold at?

Often it is a reference the seller chose, not a price this model has actually been selling at. The checkout price and this model's own recent selling prices are the facts. A chart for another size, another construction, or a similar series name answers for that other mattress.

How is a sleep trial different from a return window?

If the seller offers a sleep trial, read how many nights it lists, when the clock starts, who picks the mattress up, and whether a restocking fee applies. That trial can differ from the ordinary return window, and a final-sale label can remove both. The policy on this listing is the one that applies.

Can the base under the mattress affect the warranty?

If the sheet or the warranty lists a foundation, a box spring, or a slat gap, a base outside that line can void the warranty. Read the requirement for this model. A protection plan offered at checkout is a second purchase. It does not replace a base the warranty requires.

When should I wait on a mattress deal?

Wait when the construction or the size is unclear, when checkout only beats a reference price this model has not been selling at, or when a sale-only model has no price chart of its own. Neither the Friday nor the Monday is automatically the cheaper day. A checkout price at or under this model's own recent low, on the construction and size you will sleep on, is the one that is actually lower. If it has to arrive by a date, that date can matter more than another dip.
