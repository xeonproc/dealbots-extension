Black Friday laptop deals: the specs to check before you buy

Updated October 30, 2026

A doorbuster laptop can look like a bargain and still be a thinner computer than the name suggests. Less memory, a small or slow drive, a dimmer or softer screen, and an older processor are how that price gets low. The spec sheet, the full model number, and that model's own prices tell you whether the discount is on the laptop you actually want.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Next year the Friday moves. The checks below do not. Why doorbuster laptops often have weak specs

A sale page can use a familiar series name and a low price. The laptop in the cart can still be a cheaper configuration of that name. The parts that get cut are the ones you live with every day. A low price on a laptop that does what you need is a fair buy. A low price on a laptop that will feel cramped in a month is a different product.

Less memory

Memory is the room the laptop uses for what you have open. A smaller amount means fewer programs and browser tabs at once. When it runs out, the laptop leans on storage and feels slow. A doorbuster often lists less memory than the configuration people reviewed under a similar name.

Small or slow storage

Storage is where the programs and files live. A doorbuster sometimes uses a small drive. It sometimes uses eMMC, a kind of storage that is built into the laptop. eMMC is usually slower than an SSD, and you often cannot swap it for a larger drive later. The laptop fills up sooner, and opening programs can feel sluggish.

A softer or dimmer screen

The screen is the part you look at. A lower resolution looks softer, especially on a larger screen. A dimmer panel is harder to see in a bright room, near a window, or outside. The photo on the sale page does not tell you the resolution or the brightness. The spec sheet does.

An older-generation processor

The processor does the work. A doorbuster often uses an older generation, or a low-power chip that shares a familiar family name. It can be slower for everyday tasks than a current chip in that family. It can also be why the battery does not last as long as a review of a stronger configuration suggested. The short name in the headline is not the full processor name.

What to check on the spec sheet

Open the sheet for the model number in the cart. If you cannot find one, pause. You are being asked to buy a laptop you cannot compare. Read these lines. A missing line is an unknown. It is not a match for a better laptop with a similar name.

Memory amount, and whether it is soldered

Read how much memory the sheet lists. Then read whether you can add more later. Some laptops have a slot. Others have the memory soldered in place, which means the amount you buy is the amount you keep. If the sheet does not say, do not assume you can upgrade it. A smaller amount is easier to accept when you can add more later, and a harder limit when you cannot.

Storage type and size

Read the type and the size. An SSD is the usual storage for a laptop you will use every day. eMMC is the small, built-in kind described above. A sheet that only says "flash storage," without naming SSD or eMMC, has not told you which one it is. Then read the size. Compare it with the programs, photos, and files you will keep on the laptop. A small drive fills up. If the sheet says the drive can be replaced, that is a different laptop from one where the storage is permanent.

Processor generation

Read the full processor name, not the family name in the headline. The generation is the step in that chip's family, and it is part of the full name on the sheet. An older generation can be slower, and it can use more power, than a newer one in the same family. The name alone is not a race. A newer low-power chip can still be slower than a stronger older one. Put the two full names next to each other when you compare last year's model with this year's.

Screen resolution and brightness

Resolution is the pixel count, two numbers on the sheet. A lower count looks softer. Brightness is how well you can see the panel in a bright room. When the sheet lists it, a lower number is a dimmer screen. Sheets often give that number in nits. If brightness is missing, you do not know. A touch screen, a matte finish, or a glossy one is a separate line. Note it if it matters to how you will use the laptop.

Battery

Read the battery size if the sheet lists it, often in watt-hours, and any claim about how long a charge lasts. A runtime number is the result of the test they chose. It is not a promise about your day. A bright screen, video calls, and many open programs use the battery faster. If you will be away from a plug, a missing battery line is a reason to look for a sheet that has one.

Ports

Count the ports you will use. A USB port covers a mouse, a drive, or a phone. A video output, often HDMI, covers a separate screen. A headphone jack matters if you use wired headphones. A memory-card slot matters if you move photos that way. A laptop with few ports can still be the right one if you are willing to carry an adapter. Decide that from the sheet, before you pay.

Weight

The sheet lists a weight. Picture carrying it, with the charger. Lighter is easier to take with you. Heavier can still be the right laptop when the memory, storage, and screen are the ones you need. The number on the sheet is the one to picture. The word "thin" in a headline is not a weight.

Warranty

Read who provides the warranty, how long it lasts, and what it covers. A seasonal listing can carry a shorter warranty than the laptop you compared it with. Shorter coverage is a smaller offer, even when the price is lower. The terms on this listing are the ones that count. A length you remember from another seller does not transfer. The same care applies if the listing is refurbished or open-box, which is a different offer from new. That comparison is in refurbished vs new on Black Friday.

A sale on last year's model

A sale on last year's laptop can be a good buy. It is a sale on that laptop, not on this year's model with a similar name. Last year's model has had time to show what it usually sells for. This year's model may have only a short price history, or a list price it has not actually sold at. A big percent off this year's list price can look larger while checkout is not lower than last year's model.

Put the two spec sheets side by side. Compare memory, whether it is soldered, storage type and size, processor generation, screen resolution and brightness, battery, ports, weight, and warranty. Sometimes the newer model is a small change. Sometimes the older one gives up the memory, the storage, the screen, or the ports. The sheets say which. The series name does not.

If the sheets are close enough for how you will use the laptop, and last year's checkout price is under what that model has been selling for, last year's model can be the better buy. If you need the memory, storage, or screen that only the newer sheet lists, the lower price is a different laptop. Either way, judge the price against that model's own recent prices. How a reference price can inflate the percent off is in how to tell if a sale is real.

Match the full model number

The series name is a family. The full model number is the laptop. Memory, storage, screen, color, keyboard, and the region it was sold for can all change under one name. One different character can mark a different configuration. The same kind of mismatch, on a television, is why a Black Friday TV model number may not match the set in a review.

Write the full number from the cart, including any suffix. Match it to the spec sheet and to the price chart before you compare a price. A low for the same name with more memory, a larger drive, or a different screen is a low for that other laptop. A review of a different number describes that other laptop too. If the cart shows a shorter number than the sheet, stop and find the full one. The headline is a label. The number in the cart is the laptop you would receive.

Check this model's price history

The percent off uses a reference price the seller chose. That number may be a list price this configuration has rarely sold at. The dollar price at checkout is the fact. A badge does not say the discount is real.

Read the chart for this exact model number. About 30 to 90 days, when the chart has them. Thirty days shows a short markup just before the sale. Ninety days is the safer window when that model has already been on sale more than once. The typical price is where offers cluster. The recent low is the bottom. Those shapes are in how to read a price history chart.

A chart for a similar name, a different amount of memory or storage, or last year's model answers for that other laptop. New, open-box, and refurbished are different offers, and they do not share a chart. When the chart is short because the configuration is new, say so. A "was" price that only appears for a few days before the sale, or never appears as a selling price, makes the percent off a label. You can still set today's checkout total next to a similar model's recent low and decide whether the spec differences are worth the gap. That does not turn the other model's low into this model's low.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the spec sheet, and this model's prices.

  1. 1Does the full model number on the cart match the spec sheet and the price chart?
  2. 2How much memory is there, and is it soldered or can I add more later?
  3. 3Is the storage an SSD or eMMC, how big is it, and can it be replaced?
  4. 4Which processor generation is this, and what resolution and brightness does the screen list?
  5. 5Do the battery, ports, weight, and warranty fit how I will use this laptop?
  6. 6Is today's checkout price at or under this model's own recent low, not just under a "was" price?

When the specs match what you need, and checkout is at or under this model's own recent low, the deal is about this laptop. If the model number differs, or the chart belongs to another configuration, skip the percent off until you have this model's price.

Questions people ask

Why do Black Friday laptop deals often have weak specs?

A doorbuster laptop is often cheap because the parts cost less: less memory, small or slow storage, a lower-resolution or dimmer screen, or an older-generation processor. The series name can still match a laptop people wrote about. The spec sheet for the model in the cart is what describes the one you would receive.

What should I check on a laptop spec sheet?

Read the memory amount and whether it is soldered, the storage type and size, the processor generation, the screen resolution and brightness if they are listed, the battery, the ports, the weight, and the warranty. A missing line is an unknown. It is not a match for a better laptop with a similar name.

Is a sale on last year's laptop a good deal?

It can be, when the specs still do what you need and the checkout price is low for that exact model. Last year's laptop is a different product from this year's model with a similar name. Compare the two spec sheets, then compare today's price with what that older model has actually been selling for.

Why does the full laptop model number matter?

The full model number is the configuration. Two laptops can share a series name and differ in memory, storage, screen, or ports. A price for a different number, or for the same name with more memory, is a price for a different laptop. Match the number on the cart to the spec sheet and the price chart before you compare.

How do I tell if a laptop discount is real?

Read the price history for this exact model number, not the percent off on the page. The percent uses a reference price the seller chose. A low for a similar name, a different storage size, or last year's model belongs to that other laptop. A checkout price at or under this model's own recent low is the one that is actually lower.

What is eMMC storage on a laptop?

eMMC is a small kind of storage that is built into the laptop. It is usually slower than an SSD, and you often cannot replace it with a larger drive. If the sheet only says flash storage and does not name the type, you do not yet know which one you are buying.
