Black Friday phone deals: unlocked vs carrier, activation strings, and trade-ins

Updated November 9, 2026

A phone deal can look like a deep cut and still be a locked phone, a price that exists only if you start a line, or a trade-in quote for a phone in better shape than the one you have. The unlocked checkout price, the payments and credits you will actually owe, and this model's own prices tell you what the phone costs.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Next year the Friday moves. The checks below do not. Unlocked and carrier-locked

An unlocked phone is sold so you can bring it to a network that supports the model, without a service contract written into the price. A carrier-locked phone is tied to one network until that network's unlock rules are met. The family name can match. The offers are still different.

A retailer, including a big-box store, can sell the same model unlocked and paid at checkout. A carrier can advertise a lower number that only applies with service. Compare the two after you have added what the carrier offer costs. The lower number on the carrier page is the advertisement. The total in the next sections is the price.

A prepaid phone can look like a cash purchase and still be locked to that service for a period the listing names. Read whether it is locked, and for how long. A lock the page does not mention is an unknown. The lock is also separate from the bands. An unlocked phone that misses the bands your network uses will not get you service there. That check is below.

New line, activation, and installments

An activation string is a condition attached to the advertised price. The ones that change the cost say you must start a new line, move a number you already use onto this network, activate service, or pay the phone in installments. The price with the string and the price without it are different offers. If the page does not show the price with the string removed, you do not yet have a number to set next to an unlocked checkout.

Add the total

Write the amount due at checkout. Write each installment and how many payments there are. Write any activation fee, upgrade fee, or monthly line charge you pay because of this phone or the required line. Multiply the installment by the number of payments, then add checkout and those fees. That sum is the cost before credits.

A lower amount each month can still cost more when there are more payments, or when a fee sits outside the monthly figure. The monthly number is one line of the offer. The sum is the price you compare.

A required line

A price that requires a new line is a price for the phone plus that line. If you do not need another line, the offer is asking you to buy service you had not planned to buy. Add what that line costs for as long as the discount depends on keeping it. A line you already pay for is already in your budget. A line you would not have added is part of this deal.

Some offers require you to move a number you already use. Read whether the discount still applies if that move fails, and whether you keep the number you have. A failed move can leave you with the higher phone price and a number that did not come with you. If the page is silent, those outcomes are unknowns.

Activating the phone, or breaking the seal, can change whether you can send it back. An offer can treat an activated phone as opened, charge a restocking fee, or mark it final sale. The window, the fee, and a final-sale label are in Black Friday return policies. Read the policy linked from this cart before you activate.

Bill credits and leaving early

An instant discount reduces what you pay at checkout, or reduces the amount that is financed. When you used the prices after that discount, the instant discount is already inside the sum above. A bill credit is a later payment. It shows up on the bill, often month by month, and only while you keep the service the offer requires.

Count a bill credit for the months you expect to stay, and only if this offer says you receive it for those months. Subtract that expected credit from the sum. What remains is the cost of taking this offer. Set it next to the unlocked checkout price for the same model, the same storage, and the same condition, with no required line. The lower of those two is the cheaper way to own this phone. A percent off a list price uses a reference the seller chose. How that reference inflates the percent is in how to tell if a sale is real.

If you cancel the line, pay the phone off early, or move the number away, read what happens to credits you have not received. The remaining credits can stop. You can also still owe the rest of the phone, sometimes at the balance from before the credits. The offer should say which. If it does not, the cost of leaving is an unknown. A discount you lose by leaving was a discount on staying.

Trade-in conditions

A trade-in quote is an estimate until someone inspects the phone you send. Read the conditions on this quote. Check whether the phone must power on, whether the screen and the body must meet a condition the quote names, and whether you must remove any account that would stop the next owner from setting the phone up. A quote that requires an intact screen applies to an intact screen. After inspection the value can drop, or the phone can be sent back.

Read how the quote is paid. Money off today's checkout is in the price you pay now. A credit on a later bill follows the same rules as a bill credit above. A card you spend later is worth the part you will actually use, which is covered in gift card promos. Subtract the headline quote only after you know which of those you will receive, for the phone you actually have.

If the advertised phone price requires the trade-in, the price without a phone you are willing to send is a different offer. The new phone still has its own recent selling prices. A high quote on the old phone does not turn an ordinary price on the new one into a low. If the listing bundles the phone with a case, earbuds, or another extra, price the pieces you would keep. That comparison is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding?

Storage and last year's model

The sheet prints a storage amount. A smaller amount and a larger amount, under the same name, are different phones. Photos, video, and apps fill the smaller one sooner. If the sheet does not say you can add storage later, treat the amount in the cart as the amount you will have. A price, a review, or a chart for the larger amount describes that other phone. Match the amount on the cart to the sheet and to the chart before you compare.

Last year's model is a different phone. It has had time to show what it usually sells for. This year's model may have a short chart, or a list price it has not been selling at. A large percent off this year's list price can look bigger while checkout sits above what last year's phone actually sells for. Put the two sheets side by side and read what changed: storage, bands, and the warranty among them.

When last year's phone is enough for how you will use it, and its checkout is at or under that model's own recent prices, it can be the better buy. That checkout price belongs to last year's phone. Judge each phone against its own prices. The same kind of generation gap on a laptop is a different product, covered in Black Friday laptop deals.

The exact model and the bands

The family name is a group. The model in the cart is the phone. A sale can offer a near-identical variant: a version built for one carrier, a version sold for another country, a different storage amount, or a model with a different set of bands. The photo can match the phone people reviewed. The line in the cart is the phone you would receive.

Write the full name or model number from the cart. Match it to the spec sheet and to the price chart. A chart for another storage amount, another region, another color, or last year's model answers for that other phone. If the cart shows a shorter name than the sheet, stop and find the full one. If you cannot find a sheet for the exact model, pause.

Bands

A band is a range of radio spectrum a network uses. The phone has to support the bands that network uses where you will use it. The sheet should list the bands for this model. Compare that list with the bands your network says this model supports. A phone that works on one network can miss coverage on another. A model sold for another country can share the family name and carry a different list. A badge that only says the phone is a current-generation model has not listed the bands. A missing list is an unknown. Do not copy the bands from a different storage amount or from last year's model.

How the phone joins a network

The sheet should say whether this model has a physical card tray, a built-in SIM (an eSIM), or both. A phone with only an eSIM cannot take a physical card. Confirm the network you will use supports the method this model has. A missing line is an unknown. The warranty length on this model's sheet is the coverage you are buying. A length you remember from the reviewed model does not transfer.

Unlock policies

Unlock rules belong to the network, and they differ. A locked phone stays on that network until the conditions in its policy are met. Those conditions can include the phone being paid off, the account being in good standing, and a waiting period the policy states. A phone sold unlocked is not waiting on those conditions. A phone sold locked does not become unlocked because a different network unlocked a different model.

Read the policy for this phone and this seller before you treat a locked price as a phone you can move later. An unlock that arrives only after you have paid the installment total is a different purchase from buying unlocked today. If the policy is silent, you do not yet know whether you can leave with the phone.

A protection plan offered at checkout is a second purchase. It does not unlock the phone, and it does not change the bands. How to price that plan is in extended warranties and protection plans.

This model's price history

The percent off uses a reference price the seller chose. On a phone, that reference can be a list price this model has not been selling at, or a price that only exists before the activation strings. The dollar amount you will actually pay is the fact. For an unlocked phone, that is checkout. For a carrier offer, that is the total above, after only the credits you will receive. How a reference price inflates the percent, including a short markup just before a sale, is in how to tell if a sale is real.

Read the chart for this exact model, in this storage amount, in this condition. About 30 to 90 days, when the chart has them. Thirty days shows a short markup just before the sale. Ninety days is the safer window when that model has already been on sale more than once. The typical price is where offers cluster. The recent low is the bottom. Those shapes are in how to read a price history chart.

A chart for a similar name, a different storage amount, a locked offer, or last year's model answers for that other phone. New, open-box, and refurbished do not share a chart. How those conditions compare, including a battery a refurbished phone may not have replaced, is in refurbished vs new on Black Friday. A carrier installment price is a different figure from a point on the unlocked chart. When the chart is short because the model is new, say so. A "was" price that only appears for a few days before the sale, or never appears as a selling price, makes the percent off a label. You can set today's unlocked checkout next to a similar model's recent low and decide whether the storage and the bands are worth the gap. That does not turn the other model's low into this model's low.

When to wait

Wait when you cannot yet add the total. A missing count of payments, a credit whose rules you have not read, or a trade-in quote that does not name the condition of your phone is a reason to stop. The percent off can stay on the page while you look those up. It is not evidence that the price is low.

Wait when the model, the storage amount, or the bands are unclear, or when you cannot find a sheet for the phone in the cart. A locked phone with no unlock policy on the page is the same kind of gap. You are being asked to buy a phone you cannot compare, or a phone you may not be able to move.

Wait when today's cash checkout, or the carrier total after credits you will actually receive, sits near the typical price, or only under a reference price this model has not been selling at. A holiday label does not make that a new low. The named Friday and the named Monday are not automatically the cheaper day for this phone. Compare the two against this model's own prices, which is the point of Black Friday vs Cyber Monday. An early label, weeks before the weekend, is the same comparison, covered in early Black Friday deals.

You do not have to wait when the model and the storage match the sheet and the chart, the bands cover the network you will use, you have added the line, the credits, and the trade-in you will actually get, and that total is at or under this phone's own recent low on the same terms. A later label that only ties that price is the same price with a different headline. If the phone has to arrive by a date, the delivery date can matter more than another dip. That check is in holiday shipping cutoffs. The months when electronics commonly go on sale are in the best time to buy. The calendar is a reason to look. The total for this phone is the reason to pay.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the spec sheet, the offer's payment terms, and this model's prices.

  1. 1Is this phone unlocked, or locked to a carrier, and am I comparing it with the unlocked checkout price?
  2. 2Does the advertised price require a new line, a number move, an activation, or an installment plan?
  3. 3What do checkout, the installments, and the fees add up to, before credits?
  4. 4Which bill credits will I actually receive, and what do I still owe if I leave early?
  5. 5What condition does the trade-in quote require, and how is that value paid?
  6. 6Does the storage amount in the cart match the phone I mean, or is this last year's model?
  7. 7Does this exact model support the bands and the SIM type for the network I will use, and what does the unlock policy say?
  8. 8Is the total I will pay at or under this model's own recent low, for this storage and this condition?

When the model matches, the bands fit the network you will use, and the total you will pay is at or under this phone's own recent low on the same terms, the deal is about this phone. If the chart belongs to another storage amount, or the price only exists with a line you will not keep, skip the percent off until you have this phone's price.

Questions people ask

Is an unlocked phone the same deal as a carrier price?

An unlocked phone is a cash price for the phone. A carrier price can be the phone plus service, and it can require a new line, an activation, or installments. Those are different offers, even when the family name matches. Add what the carrier offer actually costs, then compare that total with the unlocked checkout price for the same model, storage, and condition.

How do I add up a phone discount that needs a new line or installments?

Write the amount due at checkout, each installment multiplied by the number of payments, and any activation or line fee you will pay. That sum is the cost before credits. Subtract only bill credits you will actually receive for the months you will stay. A lower amount each month, stretched over more payments, can cost more in total. The sum, after those credits, is the number to compare with an unlocked checkout price.

What is the difference between a bill credit and an instant discount?

An instant discount reduces what you pay at checkout or the amount that is financed. A bill credit arrives later, usually month by month, and only while you keep the service the offer requires. If you cancel, pay the phone off early, or move your number, remaining credits can stop, and you can still owe the rest of the phone. Read what this offer says happens if you leave.

When does a trade-in quote become the price I actually get?

A quote is an estimate until the phone you send is inspected. Read the condition it requires, including whether the phone must power on and whether the screen and body must meet a standard the quote names. The value can drop after inspection. Money off checkout, a later bill credit, and a card you spend later are different ways to be paid. Count only the value you will actually receive for the phone you have.

Does a different storage amount, or last year's model, share this phone's price?

A smaller storage amount and a larger one, under the same name, are different phones. Last year's model is a different phone too. A price, a review, or a chart for the other one answers for that other phone. Match the storage and the model in the cart to the sheet and the chart before you compare.

How do I check the model, the bands, and an unlock policy?

Match the full model in the cart to the spec sheet. The sheet should list the bands this model supports. Compare that list with the bands the network you will use says this model supports. A locked phone stays locked until the policy's conditions are met, which can include the phone being paid off and a waiting period the policy names. A missing band list or a missing unlock policy is an unknown.
