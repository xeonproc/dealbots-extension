Black Friday home fitness equipment deals: what to check before you buy

Updated November 25, 2026

A treadmill, bike, rower, dumbbell set, or rack can look like a deep cut and still be a peak horsepower number, a membership the screen needs, or a "compare at" price the model has not been selling at. The continuous-duty rating, the weight limit, and this model's own prices tell you whether the discount is on the equipment you will keep.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Next year the Friday moves. The checks below do not. Continuous-duty horsepower and peak

Continuous-duty horsepower, also printed as continuous horsepower or CHP, is what a treadmill motor is rated to hold while it runs. Peak horsepower is a short burst. Suppose a sheet prints 2.5 continuous and 3.5 peak. That pair is a sketch of two figures on one sheet, not a listing. Compare continuous with continuous. An unlabeled headline number may be only the peak. If the sheet does not say which kind it printed, you do not know.

Deck size and the user-weight limit

Belt length runs in the direction you walk, and width runs side to side. Read both. A shorter, narrower belt is a different machine even when the horsepower matches. The maximum user weight is the maker's limit for this frame, deck, and motor. A higher limit is not a wider belt. A bike or a rower has its own limit. Read the seat-height range on a bike, and the rail length or user height on a rower. A missing figure means you learn the fit by getting on it.

Folding and a machine that stays open

A folding treadmill lifts the deck and latches it. Read the folded height, width, and depth. A machine that does not fold keeps the open footprint. "Folds," without dimensions, has not told you it fits. Open size needs room to step on and off, and folded size has to fit the closet. The carton still has to pass the door and the stairs. Measure that path.

Noise, the floor, and the ceiling

If the sheet prints no noise figure, noise is unknown. A figure that leaves out the speed it was measured at is hard to compare. Shake goes into the floor, and an upstairs floor passes it to the room below. A mat is a separate purchase unless the box includes one, and it does not change the weight limit. Ceiling clearance is you on the deck with an arm raised, not the console height. Above a pull-up bar, your reach is taller than the upright.

Magnetic, air, and friction

Magnetic resistance moves magnets near a metal flywheel. Air resistance is a fan: go faster and the load rises, and the fan is also noise. Friction presses a pad on the flywheel. The pad wears, so read whether you can buy another. A rower may use a water tank instead. The sheet has to name the type. "Resistance" alone has not. Compare a magnetic machine with a magnetic one.

Flywheel weight and how far it adjusts

Compare a printed flywheel weight only with the same resistance type, and only when both sheets print one. An air machine may print a fan size instead. A count of levels means smaller steps, which matter if you will use them. Air load often changes only with your pace. "Adjustable," with no count and no range, has not said how far the load moves.

The membership the sale price skips

Some consoles show speed and resistance with no account. Some lock classes or automatic resistance behind a paid app or membership. Read what still runs with the account skipped. A trial that renews costs money unless you cancel on the printed terms. Add that price for every year you would keep it. A lower checkout plus a membership you will keep can cost more than a machine with no fee. If no price is printed, the total is unknown. A phone or tablet the app requires is another purchase when you do not have one it supports. A watch plan is a separate check in Black Friday smartwatch and fitness tracker deals.

Dumbbell steps and the heaviest setting

Read every setting, per dumbbell, and the gap between them. Suppose a sheet lists 10, 25, and 40 in one hand. That list is a sketch, not a product, and the weights between those numbers are not in the set. The maximum is the heaviest setting. Read whether it is one dumbbell or the pair. A pair total is not the weight in one hand. A stand is included only when the box list says so, and it has its own rating.

Rack rating, bench rating, and floor space

Read the rack limit and the bench limit. An incline can be rated lower than flat, so one number may not cover every position. The bar has to fit the upright spacing, and its sleeve diameter has to match your plates. Read the base width, depth, and upright height, and leave room to load a bar. Overhead work needs ceiling for you with your arms up. A rack that must be bolted needs a floor you can drill. A missing bench or bar is another price.

Curbside, in-home, and assembly

Curbside means you carry the cartons, including on stairs. In-home means someone brings them to a room the page names, and stairs may cost extra. Assembly can be included, a fee, or left to you. A fee that appears later was left out of the sale price. If the page does not say where the boxes stop, ask before you pay. A date the machine has to arrive by is in holiday shipping cutoffs.

Sending a bulky machine back

A bulky return can mean a shorter window, a restocking fee, a pickup fee, or freight you pay. Assembly can count as opened. Keep the cartons if the policy requires them. Put the fee and the return freight next to the sale price before you buy. Windows, labels, and what opened means are in Black Friday return policies. The amounts on this listing are the ones you have.

Frame, motor, parts, and labor

Frame, motor, other parts, and labor can have different lengths. The long headline number is often only the frame. Labor can be excluded, so you still pay a technician or the shipping. Read who pays to move a heavy machine, and whether a belt, a deck, or a friction pad is left out. A plan at checkout is a second purchase, compared in extended warranties and protection plans. Open-box terms can be shorter, which is part of refurbished vs new.

A two-year total, with made-up numbers

Delivery, assembly, a mat, and a membership sit on top of the sale price. Every amount below is made up. These figures are not a listing, not a typical price, and not a prediction. Replace each one with the number on the page you are reading.

Machine A checks out at $800, needs no membership, and in this sketch pays $100 for curbside delivery and $40 for a mat. You assemble it. Over two years that is 800 + 100 + 40 = $940.

Machine B checks out at $600. In-home delivery and assembly are $180. A membership at $20 a month, kept for two years, is 20 × 24 = $480. A mat is $40. Machine B comes to 600 + 180 + 480 + 40 = $1,300.

In the sketch, the lower checkout costs more. One year of that membership is 20 × 12 = $240, so machine B becomes 600 + 180 + 240 + 40 = $1,060, still higher here. Use the years you would actually keep it. If the membership price is missing, you cannot finish the sum.

The "was" price on this model

A "was," "list," or "compare at" figure is a reference the seller chose. It may never have sold, or it may include a mat or a membership this box does not. A mat or a trial in the package

Subtract the machine-only price, same model and condition. What remains is the mat, the plates, or the trial. An extra you would not buy counts as nothing. A trial you will keep belongs in the membership line above. The rest of that split is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding?

When to wait

Wait when horsepower type, belt size, the user limit, the resistance type, or the membership price is missing, or when delivery and the return cost are unknown. Wait when the cut shows only beside a "was" price. Black Friday vs Cyber Monday and early Black Friday deals cover the calendar. Buy when checkout is at or under this model's recent low after delivery, assembly, and every year of membership you would keep.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the sheet, the return policy, and this model's prices.

  1. 1Is the treadmill horsepower continuous-duty or peak, and what are the belt length and width?
  2. 2Does the user-weight limit cover me, and do the open size, the folded size, the door, and the ceiling fit?
  3. 3Is the resistance magnetic, air, friction, or water, and what flywheel weight or fan size is printed?
  4. 4How many levels does it have, and what still runs with no membership?
  5. 5What does that membership cost per year for as long as I would keep it?
  6. 6Do the dumbbell steps include the weights I want, per hand, and is a stand in the box?
  7. 7Do the rack and bench ratings, the footprint, and the bar diameter fit the plates and the ceiling?
  8. 8Where is it delivered, what does assembly cost, and what does a return cost after it is built?
  9. 9After the frame, motor, parts, labor, and shipping on a claim, is checkout at or under this model's recent low?

When those match, the deal is this machine.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between continuous-duty horsepower and peak horsepower?

Continuous-duty horsepower is what the motor is rated to hold while it runs. Peak horsepower is a short burst. Compare continuous with continuous. If the sheet does not say which number it printed, you do not know.

How do I tell whether a folding treadmill will fit?

Read the open size and the folded size, then measure the room, the ceiling with you on it, and the doorway. "Folds," without dimensions, has not told you it fits.

How do I count a required membership into the deal?

Add the membership for every year you would keep it, plus delivery, assembly, and any mat the box leaves out. A lower checkout can cost more once those lines are in. If the membership price is missing, the total is unknown.

What should I check on adjustable dumbbells?

Read each setting and the gap between settings, per dumbbell. A weight the list skips is not in the set. A pair total is not the weight in one hand, and a stand is included only when the box list says so.

What should the warranty on a treadmill or a rack cover?

Read frame, motor, other parts, and labor as separate lines. The long headline number is often only the frame. Read who pays shipping, and whether a belt, a deck, or a friction pad is excluded.

Should I trust the was price on a home fitness machine?
