Black Friday power tool deals: what to check before you buy

Updated November 19, 2026

A power tool deal can look like a deep cut and still be a bare tool, a battery from a system you do not own, or a "compare at" price the tool has not been selling at. The battery system, what the box includes, and this tool's own prices tell you whether the discount is on the drill, the saw, or the kit you meant to buy.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Next year the Friday moves. The checks below do not. One battery system

A cordless pack fits one system. Another system's pack will not run the tool, and that charger will not charge yours, even at a similar voltage. A compact line and a full-size line from the same maker can be two systems, so read the name on the pack and on the tool. Two voltages on one pack are still one system: use the number the sheet names. A higher number is not a bigger pack. A second system adds a pack, a charger, and later packs this price leaves out. A pack stored between jobs still ages.

Bare tool versus kit

Bare, or tool only, means no battery and often no charger. A kit adds the packs and charger the sheet lists. Compare bare tools of the same model with each other, and kits only when the pack count and each amp-hour rating match. Match the model number in the cart to the sheet. A bare price beside a kit's "was" price is two products. Add any pack and charger you still need before you call the bare tool cheaper. If the sheet never lists them, the contents are unknown.

Battery capacity and the charger

Amp-hours, written Ah, are the charge a pack holds at that system's voltage. More Ah is a larger pack, not one that fits other tools. One number on the page may be each pack or the total in the box. Two small packs are not one large pack. Subtract a current bare-tool price from the kit, divide by the pack count, and compare that with the same Ah pack sold alone. A one-pack charger, a multi-pack charger, and a faster charger are different. If the type is missing, add the charger you will buy.

A free tool or a free battery

A "free" tool or battery is a package you still pay for. It is a saving when you would have bought that extra on a system you will keep, and the total is under the separate prices. A pack you cannot use, a tool you will not run, or a lower Ah than you wanted is a different purchase. Count anything you would not buy as nothing. Brushed versus brushless

A brushed motor has brushes that wear. A brushless motor does not. Similar names can hide either one, so read this sheet. A blank motor line is unknown, and a review of the other motor does not carry over. A cheaper brushed tool can be the older design, not a discount on the brushless model. Power and runtime have to be printed here. Buy brushless when you wanted that model and checkout is at or under its own recent prices.

Last year's model and a new version

Last year's clearance is a sound buy when you know the year, the packs fit a system you will keep, and checkout is at or under that model's own recent low. A new version can change the motor, the size, the chuck, or the box and keep the name. Match the model number on the tool, the carton, and the cart. A percent off the new list price, printed on the old tool, is a label. A smaller pack, or packs that do not fit your older tools, is a different purchase.

Corded versus cordless

A corded tool needs an outlet and a cord that reaches. It does not need a pack. A cordless tool needs a charged pack from its own system. For occasional work next to an outlet, corded can be the whole purchase. A cordless kit for one job leaves you owning that system. Cordless fits work away from an outlet, or when you already own packs you will use on other tools. Add the packs and charger you would still buy before you compare the two prices.

Combo kits

A combo is several tools, packs, and a charger at one price. The per-tool figure falls when the count includes a light, a radio, or a drill you will not use. Price the tools you want as bare tools on your system, add the packs and the charger, and compare. Anything you would not buy counts as nothing. Two battery systems in one box are two purchases. Bit sets and piece counts

A bit set, a blade set, or a mixed case is worth the pieces you will use, not the count on the lid. Read the sizes. A count with no list is unknown, and "up to" is a ceiling. The printed value is a reference the seller chose. Compare bits you would have bought with those bits sold alone. A blade for another saw, or a bit that misses this chuck, is not part of the saving.

Warranty and registration

Read the tool warranty and the battery warranty as separate lines. Another tool's term does not transfer. The longer line can require you to register the tool, the pack, or both by a stated date. If the page is silent, you do not know if registration is required. When you expect to replace the pack, a shorter battery term is the one that matters. A plan at checkout is a second purchase, compared in extended warranties and protection plans. What you can send back is in Black Friday return policies.

Reconditioned and open-box

Reconditioned, refurbished, renewed, and open-box are the seller's labels, not one condition. Read what was tested or replaced. The pack may be the original, and the charger or case may be missing. If the pack is not on the sheet, treat it as unknown. A bare reconditioned tool and a new kit are different products. Compare this checkout with the new tool's recent prices for the same contents. The "was" price and this tool's history

A "was," "list," or "compare at" figure is a reference the seller chose. It may be a kit price beside a bare tool, or a number this model has not sold at. Compare checkout with recent selling prices for this model, these packs, and this condition. A bare tool and a kit do not share a low, and last year's model has its own chart. When to wait

Wait if the system, the pack count, the amp-hour rating, or the charger is missing. Wait if the free piece is something you would not buy, if the model or the motor is unclear, or if a reconditioned listing skips the pack or the warranty. Wait if the discount shows only beside a "was" price. Black Friday vs Cyber Monday and early Black Friday deals cover the calendar. Buy when the cart matches the sheet and checkout is at or under this model's recent low after the pieces you will still pay for.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the sheet, and this tool's prices.

  1. 1Is this the battery system I already own, or am I starting another system whose packs will not fit the tools I have?
  2. 2Does the box include the packs and the charger, or is this a bare tool I still have to complete?
  3. 3What is the pack count and the amp-hour rating of each pack, and how does that per-pack price compare with a pack sold alone?
  4. 4If something is called free, would I buy that piece anyway, on a system I will keep?
  5. 5Does the sheet say brushed or brushless, and is the model number the version I meant, including last year's clearance?
  6. 6For occasional use near an outlet, is a corded tool the smaller purchase once the packs and the charger are included?
  7. 7On a combo or a bit set, which pieces will I use, with the rest counted at nothing?
  8. 8What warranty applies to the tool and to the battery, do I have to register, and is a reconditioned pack the original one?
  9. 9Is checkout at or under this model's recent low for these contents, or only lower next to a "was" price?

When those match, the deal is this tool.

Questions people ask

Can a battery from one system run a tool from another?

Only when the pack fits that system and the voltage is the one the sheet names. A similar voltage on another system is a different pack. Its charger will not charge the packs you already own.

Is a bare tool a better price than a kit?

It is when you already have a pack and a charger that fit, or when adding those still leaves you under the kit. Compare bare with bare, and a kit with kits that include the same packs. A bare price next to a kit's was price is two products.

How do I compare kits that include different amp-hour packs?

Read the pack count and the amp-hour rating of each pack. A higher rating on the same system is a larger pack, not a different system. Two smaller packs are not one larger pack. Subtract a bare-tool price from the kit, divide by the pack count, and compare that with the same pack sold alone.

Is a free battery or a free tool a real saving?

It is when you would have bought that piece anyway, on a system you will keep, and the package costs less than the pieces you wanted. A tool you will not use, or a pack for a system you do not own, is only in the box. You still pay the package total.

Is a brushless tool always the one to buy?

Brushed and brushless are different motors, and a similar name can hide either one. Read the sheet. A lower price on a brushed tool can be the older model. Buy the motor you meant when that model's checkout is at or under its own recent prices.

Should I trust the was price on a power tool?

Compare checkout with this tool's recent selling prices for the same contents and condition. A was, list, or compare-at figure is a reference the seller chose. If it never shows up as a selling price, the percent is a label.
