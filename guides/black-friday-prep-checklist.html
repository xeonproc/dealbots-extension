Black Friday 2026 prep checklist: what to do in the 5 weeks before

Updated October 23, 2026

The useful work happens before the ads. A want list, a cap on what you will pay, and a note of the normal price turn the weekend into a comparison you can make at checkout.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. This checklist starts five weeks earlier, on October 23, and runs through November 30. The dates follow Thanksgiving, so next year the Friday moves. The order of the work stays the same. How to use these five weeks

Do the weeks in order when you can. The want list and the caps come first, because every later check needs an item and a ceiling to compare. If you open this page later in November, write the list and the caps the same day, then start with the week you are in. A late note still counts. A price you never wrote down becomes a guess on November 27.

Each cap is the most you will pay for that item after codes, shipping, and any required add-on, before a mail-in rebate you might not receive. Keep a total for the whole list, so five items that each look fine still fit the money you planned to spend. The cap is a ceiling you chose while the weekend was still quiet.

Week by week

October 23 through November 30, 2026. Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. Each week has one job. None of them promises that a price will fall.

October 23–29: the want list and the caps

Write the items you intend to buy. Name each one specifically enough to compare later: the model, the size, the color, the count, and the condition if a refurbished or open-box version is in the running. A line that says "a laptop" will match too many offers. A line that names the model matches one.

Put a cap next to each item, and mark whether it is a replacement you already needed or something you could skip. If two items share the same money, decide which one wins before an ad asks you to buy both. Leave gifts on the list too, with the date they need to arrive, so a return window can still be open on the day they are opened.

October 30–November 5: the normal price

Start watching the items on the list. Write the selling price, the date, and the exact variant. Check more than once, on different days. The price that shows up most often is the normal price. One look can land on a one-day dip or a one-day markup and treat that spike as the usual level.

Set the crossed-out number aside while you do this. The normal price is what the page is asking shoppers to pay on an ordinary day. A sale badge in late October or early November can still be that ordinary price. You are collecting a baseline. The decision comes later, when you can set a weekend total next to these notes.

How to spot an inflated "was" price

The number next to "was," "list," or "compare at" is a reference the seller chose. The page does not have to show that anyone paid it, or that it matched this count, size, color, and condition. Raise that reference, leave the selling price where it was, and the badge shows a deep cut while the amount due barely moves.

The usual shape is a short step up, then a discount back to the old everyday price. The percent uses the top of the step. The amount due matches the price from before the jump. Sometimes the selling price never moves at all: the reference sits above the normal price, and the badge stays on.

Compare the checkout total with the prices you wrote down, and with the low over the last 60 to 90 days when you can see that far. Sixty days gets past a short markup. Ninety days is the safer window when the item already goes on sale every month or two. A price at or under that recent low, for the same item, is a real candidate. A price that only beats the "was" price is the regular price with a holiday label. Match the count, size, color, condition, and model, or the low belongs to a different product.

November 6–12: price history and alerts

If a price history chart exists for the exact item, read it this week. Find where the line spends most of its time. That level is the typical price, and it should agree with the normal price in your notes. Write down the recent low as well. A later Black Friday price has to come in under those figures. The list price is only the seller's reference.

A price alert can watch an item you chose and tell you when a number moves. Set it on the exact variant on your list, at a dollar amount you would actually pay: at or under your cap, and under the normal price. When it fires, open checkout and compare the amount due with your notes. An alert pointed at a different size, a different model, or a "was" price will call you about an offer you did not mean to track. The alert is the reminder. The comparison is still yours to make.

No chart and no alert? Keep the dated notes. Two or three dollar amounts from this month are more useful than a percent copied off the product page. Search the product name plus "price" and read recent posts from deal communities when you want more points. Keep the dollar amounts. Set the copied percentages aside.

November 13–19: returns and price adjustments

Read the return policy and any price-adjustment policy before you need them. Both are set by the seller, and both often change around this weekend. A usual return window may shrink, stretch into the new year, or leave out opened items, limited-quantity offers, and anything marked final sale. For a gift, the window has to still be open on the day it is opened. A restocking fee, a missing serial number, or a required box can make a free return cost money.

A price adjustment is the seller's rule for a later drop on the same item. Some refund the difference for a limited time. Others pause that rule for the holiday, leave out limited-quantity offers, or only adjust a later price from the same seller. Read the current policy for the offer you might buy, and save the checkout page. If the offer is excluded, a lower price the following week belongs to the next buyer. Do this for every item still on the list, including the limited-quantity offer. That is the offer a holiday policy most often leaves out. Holiday return windows and lookalike model numbers are covered in are Black Friday deals actually real?

November 20–26: what you will skip

Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. This is the week the ads get louder, including early promotions that borrow the Black Friday name. How to judge a label that shows up even earlier, in late October and early November, is in early Black Friday deals: buy now or wait?. Take each item still on your list and set today's checkout total next to three numbers you already have: your cap, the normal price, and the recent low. If the total is at or under the cap and at or under the recent low, you can buy it this week. A limited quantity can be gone by Friday. Once the price already clears both tests, waiting is a bet that the same item will still be there.

If today's total matches the normal price, a new headline has not made a discount. Leave it, and check again on November 27 and November 30. Many offers run through the whole stretch and change the name each day. When the amount due stays put, you are looking at the same price. The plain comparison of the two named days is in Black Friday vs Cyber Monday.

Cross off anything that was never on the list. A doorbuster you did not plan is an impulse buy with a deadline on it. The short quantity and the early start are built to rush the decision. The default for an item that is not on the list is to leave it. Add it only when you can name the model, put a cap on it, and still see a price at or under the recent low. Doing that in a hurry, against a countdown, is how the cap gets skipped.

November 27–30: a day-of checklist

Black Friday is Friday, November 27. Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. The Saturday and Sunday between them often carry the same offers. Run this list at checkout, for the exact item, on the day you are buying. If Friday and Monday both show a price, run it twice. The lower honest total is the one that counts.

  1. 1Is this item on my want list?
  2. 2What will I pay after codes, shipping, and any required add-on, and is that total at or under my cap?
  3. 3Is today at or under the normal price and the recent low I wrote down, or only under a "was" price?
  4. 4Is this the same count, size, color, condition, and model I watched?
  5. 5Do the return rules and any price-adjustment policy still cover this offer, including a gift?
  6. 6If this is a doorbuster that was never on my list, will I close the page?

Write down the checkout total and the date when you buy, and again if you are still watching that item through November 30. The before-and-after comparison is those notes side by side: the normal price from late October or early November, the Friday total, and the Monday total. A weekend price that ties the normal price was the same price with a new name. A weekend price under the recent low and under your cap is the price the earlier weeks were for.

When the item is on the list, checkout clears the cap and the recent low, the model matches, and the return terms leave you a way out, buy it. If the recent low is unclear, skip the percent off. Questions people ask

When should I start preparing for Black Friday 2026?

Five weeks before is enough time to write a want list, set a cap for each item, and learn the normal price. In 2026 that week begins October 23. Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. If you start later, write the list and the caps first, then pick up the week you are in.

How do I spot an inflated was price?

Compare the checkout total with the price the same item has been selling for, including the recent low over the last 60 to 90 days. A was price that was raised and then discounted back to the old everyday price shows a large percent off and the same amount due.

Are price alerts enough to know a Black Friday deal is real?

A price alert tells you a number moved. Treat that as a prompt to compare the amount due with your cap, the normal price, and the recent low for that exact item.

Should I wait for Black Friday to buy?

Buy when the checkout total is at or under your cap and at or under the recent low. Prices often move in the weeks before November 27 and again through November 30. A Friday price that matches a total you already wrote down is that earlier price with a new headline.

What should I check on Black Friday and Cyber Monday?

Confirm the item is on your want list, the checkout total is at or under your cap, the price beats the recent low, the model matches the one you watched, and the return and price-adjustment rules still cover the offer. Skip a doorbuster that was never on your list.
