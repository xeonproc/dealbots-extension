Black Friday camera deals: what to check before you buy

Updated November 17, 2026

A camera deal can look like a deep cut and still be a kit lens you would not keep, last year's body, or a video mode that crops and then stops. The mount, what the box includes, the warranty, and this body's own prices tell you whether the discount is on the camera you meant to buy.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Next year the Friday moves. The checks below do not. See how to tell if a sale is real and how to read a price history chart. Kit lenses, used bodies, and the card the box leaves out are in Related guides.

Body-only and the kit lens

A body-only listing is the camera without a lens. A kit adds a lens at the same checkout. The bundled lens is usually a zoom for an everyday range, and the widest aperture often gets darker as you zoom. The sheet names the range and the aperture. It is a starter lens, not the lens in a review of a different bundle.

The kit is worth it when you need a lens for this mount, you would buy that range anyway, and the kit costs less than the body plus that lens bought separately, in the same condition. Skip it when you already own lenses for the mount, or when you would replace the kit lens immediately. A lens you will not use is not part of the saving. Price only the pieces you would buy. That method is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding? This page does not repeat it.

Last year's body on clearance

The family name is a group. The body in the cart is the camera. Compare last year's sheet with the new one. Read the sensor size, not only the pixel count. Read whether autofocus tracks a face or an eye in the video mode you will use. Read the video limits below, and the battery, which may not fit across years. A rated shot count is the result of a test. It is not a promise about your day.

A large percent off an older body is not automatically a deal. That body already has prices it has been selling at, and a percent off a list price can look deep while checkout sits near them. The older body is the better buy when the sheets are close enough for how you will shoot and checkout is at or under that body's own recent prices. It is a different camera when you needed the autofocus, the video mode, or the battery only the newer sheet lists. Each body has its own chart.

Sensor size and the lens mount

The sheet names the sensor size. A full-frame body and a smaller sensor are different cameras, even under a similar name. A lens made for the smaller sensor can fail to cover the larger one, or the body can crop to the area the lens covers. The sheet says which. A size from another body in the family does not transfer.

The mount is the bayonet the lens locks into. A lens does not fit a different mount unless an adapter says it will. The body is the first cost. The lenses you add later are the rest, so a low price on a mount with few lenses you want can cost more over the years you keep the camera. An adapter can add length and can drop autofocus, stabilization, or an exposure mode. Read what still works. If the adapter's sheet is silent, that is unknown. Price the adapter when you do not already own one the sheet accepts.

Video limits, heat, crop, and frame rate

Read the recording-time limit for the resolution and frame rate you will use. Some bodies stop at a time the sheet prints, then start a new clip, or they stop until the body cools. A limit on a lower resolution does not answer for a higher one. The sheet may say heat ends a clip early. A time from a cool room is not a time in the sun. If the sheet gives no time, the limit is unknown.

A higher resolution or a higher frame rate can record from a smaller part of the sensor, so the same lens sees a tighter angle than it does for a still. The sheet says whether that mode uses the whole sensor. A frame rate written as up to is a ceiling. The line under it can drop the resolution, crop the sensor, or shorten the clip. Read the line for the mode you will record.

Imported units and the warranty

An authorized unit is one the maker warrants where you live. A grey-market or imported unit can be the same body with a warranty the maker will not honor there, a manual or menus in another language, or a plug that does not match the wall. That difference can be why the price is lower. Read who services it, in which country, and in which language the manual and the menus ship.

If the listing is silent, coverage is unknown. A length from an authorized listing does not transfer. The imported unit can still be a sound buy once you have read the difference and accepted it. A protection plan in the cart is a separate purchase. How to compare it is in extended warranties and protection plans. The two offers do not share a chart.

Action cameras and compact cameras

An action camera is made to mount on a helmet, a bar, or a chest strap, usually in a frame or a housing. A compact or vlogging camera is smaller, sometimes with a lens you cannot change and a screen that faces you. A built-in lens is not a mount you can change later. Read whether a microphone plugs in. A screen that does not face you is a different camera for someone speaking to the lens.

The photo can show a mount, a grip, a microphone, or a spare battery the cart leaves out. If the sheet does not list it, it is not included. Add the mounts, housings, and batteries you will keep buying. Cloud backup or automatic highlights can require a subscription for as long as you keep them. A renewing trial costs money unless you cancel on the printed terms. The sale price is the camera. Count those extras the way Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding? describes, and leave out a mount you will not use.

Drones

A drone is not a camera body. Registration and remote-ID rules exist in many places. Check the rules where you will fly before you treat a low price as a camera you can use there.

Extra batteries often pad the package. Price the aircraft and the batteries you will fly. A care plan or a replacement plan is a separate purchase. How to compare that plan with the warranty already on the listing is in extended warranties and protection plans. How to split the battery bundle is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding?

Memory cards and the other extras

The sheet names the card type and the speed class video needs. Stills can be fine on a slower card. A video mode can refuse to record, or drop frames, below that class. The card is often left out of the box. Add it.

A card priced far under other cards of the same labeled class is a reason to read who warrants it. A card that fails can take the photos with it. If the speed is missing, you do not know whether video will run. A strap, a spare battery, a charger, or a bag counts only when the sheet lists it. The camera's chart is not the card's chart.

Used, refurbished, and open-box

A used, refurbished, or open-box body can beat new, or lose once new is on sale. What those labels change is in refurbished vs new. Read the shutter count when the listing prints one, and set it next to the mechanical-shutter rating if the sheet lists one. A missing count or a missing rating is an unknown. An electronic shutter does not share that rating. Read which shutter this body uses.

Read whether the listing says the sensor was inspected, and match the lens, the battery, and the charger to the photo. A missing piece is a different offer. Mounting a lens to test the body can count as opened. That window is in Black Friday return policies. New, open-box, refurbished, and used do not share a chart.

When to wait

Wait if the body, the mount, the sensor size, or the included lens is missing. Wait if the video limit, the crop, or the frame rate you will use is not on the sheet. Wait if the listing does not say who services the body where you live, if a card or a battery you need is unpriced, or if you have not checked the drone rules where you will fly. Wait if checkout is lower only next to a reference price. Compare Friday and Monday in Black Friday vs Cyber Monday, and an early label in early Black Friday deals. Buy when the cart matches the sheet and checkout is at or under this body's recent low, after the lens, the card, and the fees you will keep. A delivery date is in holiday shipping cutoffs.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the sheet, and this body's prices.

  1. 1Is this the body I mean, sold alone or with the kit lens the sheet names?
  2. 2If it is a kit, is checkout lower than the body plus a lens I would actually buy?
  3. 3On last year's body, how do the sensor, the autofocus, the video limits, and the battery compare?
  4. 4Do the mount and the sensor size match the lenses I will use, and what does an adapter drop?
  5. 5For the video mode I will use, what are the time limit, the heat warning, the crop, and the frame rate?
  6. 6Who warrants this body where I live, and in which language do the manual and the menus ship?
  7. 7Are the card, the batteries, the mounts, and any subscription I will keep already in the total?
  8. 8On a used body, what shutter count and return window does this listing print, and is checkout at or under this body's recent low?

When those match, the deal is this camera.

Questions people ask

Is a kit lens a better deal than the body alone?

The kit is the better buy when you need a lens for this mount, you would buy that range anyway, and the kit costs less than the body plus that lens sold separately. A lens you will not keep is not part of the saving. How to price the pieces is in the bundles guide.

Is a large percent off last year's camera automatically a deal?

It is a deal when checkout is at or under that older body's own recent prices. A newer sheet does not transfer its autofocus, video, or battery to the older body. Another generation has its own chart.

Does a cheaper body stay cheaper once I buy lenses?

The body is the first cost. Lenses for its mount are the rest. A low price on a mount that does not take the lenses you will use, or that needs an adapter to focus, can cost more over the years you keep it.

What should I read in the video specs?

Read the recording-time limit, any heat warning, whether the mode crops the sensor, and the frame-rate line for the mode you will record. A figure written as up to is a ceiling.

Is an imported camera the same as one sold with a local warranty?

The body can match and the warranty can differ. Read who services it where you live, and which language the manual and the menus ship in. Silence on the listing means the coverage is unknown.

What should I check on a used or refurbished camera?

Read the shutter count when the listing prints one, and compare it with the rating on the sheet if the sheet lists a rating. Match the lens, the battery, and the charger to the photo. The return window is the one on this listing.
