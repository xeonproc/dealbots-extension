Sensor resolution and an upscaled "4K" file

A file labeled 4K can be a smaller sensor image stretched up to that size. Read the sensor resolution, or a line that says the sensor records at the file's resolution, for the front, the rear, and the cabin. A "4K" headline about the front has not described the other channels.

Front, rear, and cabin

Front watches the road, rear watches behind, and cabin watches the seats. The sheet has to say which of those ship in the box. A rear camera and cable sold on their own are another purchase. Each channel has its own resolution, angle, and night image.

Night and field of view

At night, compare the sensor and the aperture. Aperture is an f-number, and a smaller f-number lets in more light. "Night vision," with no aperture, is a slogan. Infrared can light a cabin, and on a front camera it can reflect off the windshield. Read which channel uses it.

Field of view is degrees. Wider shows more of the next lane and makes a plate ahead smaller. The sheet should say horizontal, vertical, or diagonal. Diagonal is the longer number, so read the rear angle separately.

Supercapacitor or battery

The camera sits on glass that gets hot and cold. A lithium battery holds a longer charge and can swell or lose capacity there. A supercapacitor holds a short charge, enough to finish saving a file when power cuts, and it tolerates that heat and cold better. It does not keep recording for hours after you park. The sheet has to say which this model uses.

Parking mode and the hardwire kit

Parking mode records with the ignition off, usually after motion or a bump, so it needs power from the car. A hardwire kit taps a fuse. A low-voltage cutoff stops the draw while the car can still start. The kit and a shop install are often extra. Add both to the sale price.

The memory card

The camera overwrites the oldest files whenever it has power. Look for an endurance line, such as hours of recording, rather than capacity alone. Speed class is the same check as on a still or action camera in Black Friday camera deals. The card is often missing from the box.

GPS, the app, and a cloud fee

GPS records speed and location, either built in or as a module sold separately. Clip playback in an app can be free, while cloud storage, alerts, or remote viewing can require a plan. Read what remains if you never pay, and add a renewing trial for as long as you would keep it. A doorbell or home security camera is a different device, in Black Friday smart home device deals.

Jump starters

Peak amps are a brief burst. Cranking or rated amps are closer to the current while the engine turns, so compare the same kind of number and match any gas or diesel claim to your engine. Watt-hours equal volts times amp-hours. Read reverse-polarity, spark-proof, short-circuit, and over-current lines separately, and follow the manuals in the car and the box. This page does not give connection steps.

Tire inflators

Duty cycle is how long it runs before it must cool, as minutes on and off or a percent. A missing duty cycle leaves that limit unknown. Use the built-in gauge when the sheet states an accuracy, such as plus or minus a PSI amount, and check it once against a gauge you trust.

Cordless power is this unit's battery. Read its capacity and any tires-per-charge test. A 12-volt plug draws from the car and can drain it with the engine off. A pack shared with a drill is in Black Friday power tool deals.

Phone projection and a head unit

A wireless adapter only works if the car already supports wired phone projection to its screen. The sheet has to list your phone and this car's year, make, and model.

A head unit replaces the radio. Add this vehicle's dash kit, harness, any steering-wheel or antenna piece, and the install. Pulling the radio to return it can count as opened, in Black Friday return policies.

Car charger watts

A single watt number may be the total shared by all ports, or one port's maximum while the others are empty. Read the table for the devices you will charge together. USB-C and USB-A are different ports, and a cable left out of the box is another price. With no table, the shared figure is unknown.

A two-year total, with made-up numbers

The card, the hardwire kit, install, and a cloud plan sit on the sale price. This example is hypothetical. Every amount below is made up. These figures are not a listing, a typical price, or a prediction. Replace each one with the number on your page.

Camera A checks out at $100. In this sketch the card costs $20 and is not in the box. You mount it, with no parking mode and no fee: 100 + 20 = $120.

Camera B checks out at $70 and includes a rear camera. Card $20, hardwire kit $30, shop install $80. Cloud at $5 a month for two years is 5 × 24 = $120. Camera B is 70 + 20 + 30 + 80 + 120 = $320.

In the sketch the lower sticker costs more. Without the cloud, Camera B is $200, still higher. Count the years you would pay, and add a missing clamp set, hose, or harness the same way.

Returns, warranty, and the "was" price

Hardwiring, adhesive left on the glass, or a head unit in the dash can count as opened. The window can be shorter, a restocking fee can apply, or the return can be refused. Read that rule before you install, and put the fee beside the sale price. The policy details are in Black Friday return policies.

The camera, the battery or capacitor, and the card can have different warranty lengths. Read who ships a claim. A plan at checkout and open-box terms are linked below.

A "was," "list," or "compare at" figure is the seller's reference. Compare checkout with recent prices for this model and these contents. Subtract the camera-only price from a bundle. An extra you would not buy counts as nothing.

When to wait

Wait if resolution, channels, the power source, or card endurance is missing, or if a kit, install, or cloud fee you would keep has no price. Wait if a jump starter shows only peak amps, an inflator skips duty cycle, or an adapter omits your car and phone. The calendar sits in Black Friday vs Cyber Monday and early Black Friday deals. Buy when checkout, after those extras, is at or under this model's recent low.

A short checklist

Answer these before you pay.

  1. 1Sensor resolution on every channel, or an upscaled front file?
  2. 2Which cameras are included, and is each angle horizontal, vertical, or diagonal?
  3. 3Supercapacitor or battery, and does parking mode include a cutoff kit?
  4. 4Is the card included, with the endurance rating this camera needs?
  5. 5What do a cloud plan and GPS cost for the time I would keep them?
  6. 6Peak or rated amps, and do the watt-hours match my engine?
  7. 7Duty cycle, gauge accuracy, and watts per port versus the shared total?
  8. 8Does the adapter or head unit fit this car and phone after install?

Questions people ask

Is a "4K" dash cam actually recording at 4K?

The file can be 4K wide because the camera scaled up a smaller sensor image. Read the sensor resolution on every channel, or a line that says the sensor matches the file.

Supercapacitor or battery?

A supercapacitor finishes saving a file when power cuts and handles parked heat and cold better than a lithium battery, which holds a longer charge. Hours of parking mode need the power source the sheet names.

What should I add to the sale price?

Add a card the box omits, the hardwire kit, install, and a cloud fee for as long as you would keep it. The dollar figures above are made up. A missing price leaves the total unknown.

How do I compare jump-starter amps?

Peak amps are a short burst. Rated or cranking amps are closer to what the engine sees. Match any gas or diesel claim to your engine. Capacity is watt-hours: volts times amp-hours.

Should I trust the was price?

Compare checkout with recent prices for this model and these contents. A was, list, or compare-at figure is the seller's reference. If it never sold, the percent is a label.
