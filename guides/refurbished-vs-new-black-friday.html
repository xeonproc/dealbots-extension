Refurbished vs new on Black Friday: when refurbished is the better deal

Updated October 29, 2026

A refurbished, open-box, or renewed unit can sit beside a new one and look like the better Black Friday buy. It is the better buy only after you match the product, use the new item's typical selling price instead of its list price, and read the warranty and return terms on each listing.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Next year the Friday moves. The checks below do not. What the condition labels generally mean

New, open-box, refurbished, renewed, and certified refurbished are the seller's labels, and the definitions vary. Read this listing. A meaning you remember from another seller does not apply here.

New

New generally means an unused unit in the original packaging, with the warranty that seller attaches to a new item. The same model from another seller can still include different parts and different terms.

Open-box

Open-box generally means opened packaging or a return. It may be unused, briefly used, or missing a cable or a manual. If the contents are not listed, you do not know. That is not the same as a unit someone inspected and repaired.

Refurbished, renewed, and certified

Refurbished generally means a used unit that was inspected and may have been repaired. The company that made the product, the seller, or someone else may have done that work, and those are different promises. Renewed is a seller's name for the same idea. Certified means that seller's program. Read what it claims to test and replace. A grade is that seller's scale too. One top grade can allow more wear than another's, so use the description and the photos.

Compare the real price gap

Refurbished is often shown under a crossed-out list price or "was" price. The seller chose that number, and the new item may rarely have sold at it. The percent off is not the gap. Start from the checkout total, the same test as in how to tell if a sale is real.

Read about 30 to 90 days for the new item: the same model, size, color, storage, and parts. Thirty days shows a markup just before the sale. Ninety days helps when the item goes on sale often. The typical price is where offers cluster. The recent low is the bottom. Those shapes are in how to read a price history chart.

Write both checkout totals after codes and shipping, before a rebate you might not receive. Beating the list price while sitting near the typical new price is not a win. Landing under the typical price and under today's new total is a real gap.

Different storage, a missing accessory, or another model number is a different item, including a television whose model number does not match the reviewed set. Keep new and refurbished on separate charts. Read the refurbished listing's own history when it has any. DealBots shows price history so you can see whether a deal is genuinely low.

Warranty and return windows to check

The warranty and the return window are part of the price. Narrower coverage, or a harder return, makes the cheaper unit a different offer even when the model matches.

Read who provides the warranty, what it covers, and what it skips. Parts, a battery, and labor can differ. It may come from the seller, the company that made the product, or someone else, and it may start on a different date than the new unit's. Do not copy a length from another seller. The policy on this offer is the source.

Read whether this condition can be returned, when the window starts, what must come back, and whether a restocking fee applies. Holiday rules often differ for open-box and refurbished units. Final sale means no return. A gift's window has to be open on the day it is opened. If the listing is silent, do not assume the new-item rules. Save the policy page.

Where refurbished tends to make sense

Refurbished tends to fit when you wanted this product new, the wear is described, and the warranty you read is enough. A previous model you want, under its usual new price, is a common case, as is a device with no sealed battery or a battery the listing says was replaced. Open-box fits when the unit is complete, barely used, returnable, and clearly under the typical new price.

Where to be careful

Batteries

Phones, tablets, laptops, and wireless headphones depend on a battery a refurbished unit may not have replaced. Read whether it is new, tested, or original, and whether the warranty covers it. Sellers do not report remaining life in one shared way. A small gap is a weak reason to guess.

Wear items

A motor that runs for hours, a surface that rubs, or a part people replace on a schedule can pass a power-on test and still be half worn. "Inspected" does not mean those parts are new.

Hygiene items

Items that touch skin or are used for personal care are a weak refurbished buy. A cleaning line is the seller's claim, not a shared standard. Once new is at a real sale price, buy new. Final sale on these is a reason to pass.

When a sale on new erases the gap

On an ordinary week, refurbished wins when new sits at its typical price and the refurbished total is clearly lower on terms you accept. A real Black Friday low on the new unit can erase that.

Compare the two checkout totals after the new sale starts. A wide gap against the list price can shrink to a small one. Close totals often favor new, because you are paying nearly the same for terms you may prefer. Refurbished still wins when it stays clearly cheaper and the model matches.

A badge does not settle it. If the new sale only cuts a list price and checkout stays near the typical price, refurbished can remain the lower real price. Read the chart. Compare Friday and Monday in Black Friday vs Cyber Monday if you can wait. Price an open-box unit inside a bundle on its own.

A short checklist

Answer these from the two listings and the new item's prices, not from the badge.

  1. 1What does this seller mean by the condition, and what was tested, replaced, or left as it arrived?
  2. 2Is this the same model I would buy new, with the same storage and included parts?
  3. 3What is the new item's typical price and recent low, ignoring the list or "was" price?
  4. 4What will I pay today for this unit, and for new, after codes and shipping?
  5. 5Who warrants each one, what is covered, and what is the return window for this condition?
  6. 6If a battery, wear, or hygiene matters, does the listing answer that, and is the real gap still wide enough?

Buy refurbished when the model matches, the total is under today's new price and the typical new price, and you accept the terms. If the sale on new closed the gap, or the listing hides what was replaced, buy new or skip it.

Questions people ask

Is refurbished a better deal than new on Black Friday?

Refurbished is the better deal when checkout is clearly under what the same model costs new today and under the new item's typical selling price, and you have read a warranty and a return window you can accept. A price that only looks low next to the new item's list price is not that gap. A Black Friday sale on the new unit can close it.

What do refurbished, open-box, and renewed mean?

They are the seller's labels, and the definitions vary by seller. Open-box generally means the packaging was opened or the item was returned. Refurbished and renewed generally mean a used unit that was inspected and sometimes repaired. Certified means certified under that seller's own program. Read what this listing says was tested, replaced, and included.

How do I compare a refurbished price with a new one?

Use the new item's typical selling price and recent low, for the same model and included parts, not the list or was price. Then compare today's refurbished checkout total with today's new checkout total, after codes and shipping. A chart that mixes new and refurbished prices is a chart for more than one product.

Can a Black Friday sale on new erase the refurbished advantage?

Yes. When the new unit's sale price falls close to the refurbished price, the warranty and return terms on the new unit can make new the better buy. If the new sale only discounts a high list price and checkout stays near the typical price, the refurbished unit can still be cheaper. Compare the two checkout totals.

What should I check in the warranty and the return window?

For each listing, read who provides the warranty, what it covers, and how returns work for this condition, including when the window starts and whether a restocking fee or a final-sale label applies. Terms vary by seller and often change for the holiday. The policy on this offer is the one that counts.

Which products call for extra care when buying refurbished?

Be careful with devices that depend on a battery the listing does not describe, with products whose parts wear out in ordinary use, and with items used for hygiene. A lower price can be a unit that is already partway through its life, or a unit you would rather buy new. Read what was replaced, and skip a final-sale item in a hygiene category.
