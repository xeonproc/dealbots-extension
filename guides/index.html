Shopping guides

Updated October 27, 2026

A sale label is a claim. These guides are the checks behind it: the checkout price, the recent low, and the fine print.

The first guide is for late October and early November, when stores start using the Black Friday name weeks before the day itself. In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. The other guides cover that weekend, including whether a bundle is real savings or padding, the rest of the year, how to read a sale on an ordinary week, what a countdown or a low-stock badge does and does not tell you, and what to do if the price drops after you have already paid.

DealBots is a free browser extension that checks price history on one pick a day. The pick comes from what deal-hunter communities are upvoting. A price that only looks low next to a fake "was" price is filtered out, and the pick explains why it made the cut. There is no account and no tracking.

  1. Early Black Friday deals: buy now or wait?

    Stores put a Black Friday label on prices weeks before November 27, 2026, and price history is how you decide what to buy now and what to leave until the weekend.

  2. Countdown timers and 'only 3 left' badges: is Black Friday urgency real?

    A countdown, a low-stock badge, or a sale-ends-tonight banner rushes the decision. Price history is how you tell a real low from the ordinary price with a clock on it.

  3. Are Black Friday deals actually real?

    Some doorbusters are a real low, and others repeat a recent selling price under a holiday label, so compare checkout with the recent low, the model number, and the return rules.

  4. Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding?

    A bundle can be a lower checkout total or padding on extras you would not buy. Price each piece, the main item's history, and the per-unit cost.

  5. Black Friday vs Cyber Monday: which one has the real deals?

    Neither named day is automatically cheaper, so compare the item you want on both days with the price it has actually been selling for.

  6. Black Friday 2026 prep checklist: what to do in the 5 weeks before

    A want list, a cap on what you will pay, and a note of the normal price turn the weekend into a comparison you can make at checkout.

  7. The price dropped right after I bought it. Can I get the difference back?

    The price dropped after you bought it. A price adjustment, a return and rebuy, or card price protection may cover the difference, and each one depends on the current terms.

  8. The best time to buy almost anything: a month-by-month guide (2026-2027)

    A month-by-month look at when categories commonly go on sale, from the late-November shopping weekend through the following October.

  9. How to tell if a sale is real

    A sale badge is only as honest as the reference price behind it, so compare checkout with the recent low, the per-unit price, and the coupon fine print.

  10. How to read a price history chart

    A price history chart is a line of prices shoppers were offered, and the typical price and the recent low tell you whether today's price is actually lower.
