Black Friday soundbar and speaker deals: what to check before you buy

Updated November 21, 2026

A soundbar or speaker deal can look like a deep cut and still be a bar without the subwoofer, a connection your television does not have, or a "compare at" price the model has not been selling at. The channel count, what the box includes, and this model's own prices tell you whether the discount is on the sound you will hear in your room.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Next year the Friday moves. The checks below do not. Channel counts, the .1, and height

A soundbar is built to sit with a television. A multi-room speaker joins other speakers over Wi-Fi. A portable speaker is the one you carry. Those are different products, and the channel count belongs to the soundbar and to a home speaker set. A passive speaker needs an amplifier the box may leave out. A powered speaker has amplification in the cabinet. Read which one the sheet describes. If it is silent, you do not know what else you would still buy.

The first number is how many main speakers the system claims. A 2.0 is left and right, and bass comes from the drivers in the bar. A 2.1 is that pair plus a .1. A 3.1 adds a center. A 5.1 adds two surround speakers. The .1 is the low-frequency channel, the subwoofer part of the count. It is a label for that channel. It does not, by itself, mean a separate subwoofer is in the carton. That check is below.

A center channel is where film dialogue is usually mixed. A 2.0 or 2.1 bar has no separate center. Voices come from the left and right together, or from processing that pulls them toward the middle. A third number, when the sheet lists one, such as 5.1.2, is height channels: sound meant to come from above you. Those can be upfiring drivers on the bar or separate speakers placed high. The sheet says which. The two surround speakers in a 5.1 count may be in the box, or they may be a pair you add later. A count that says "ready" is a system the bar can reach with speakers you do not have yet.

Virtual surround and upfiring drivers

Virtual surround is processing. It uses the drivers in the bar to suggest width, and to suggest sound from beside you or behind you. Real surround is separate speakers you place around the room. A bar with virtual surround and a bar that includes rear speakers are different products, even when both say 5.1 on the carton. Read the in-the-box list. Rear speakers you place behind the seats are real surround. A mode with a name like virtual, or a line that says the bar creates surround from the front, is processing.

Upfiring drivers point at the ceiling so the sound bounces down toward you. They depend on a ceiling that reflects. A high ceiling, a vaulted ceiling, or a soft ceiling can weaken that bounce. Speakers at ear height beside or behind you are a different setup, and they do not need the ceiling to do the job. If the sheet says height channels and does not say whether the drivers fire up or whether separate speakers are in the box, you do not know which one you would be buying. Hearing it in your room is the later check.

Whether the subwoofer is in the box

Some bars produce the low end from drivers inside the bar. Some include a separate subwoofer, wired or wireless. Some list a subwoofer as an add-on. "Includes subwoofer" on the in-the-box list is the one that arrives. "Subwoofer ready," "expandable," or "compatible with a subwoofer" is a subwoofer sold separately. Add that price when you want the low end and the carton does not include it. A wireless subwoofer from another system may not pair. Read the sheet for the subwoofer this model accepts.

A photo can show a subwoofer the cart does not include. Match the model number on the carton, the sheet, and the cart, then read the contents for that number. A bar-only listing and a bar-plus-subwoofer listing do not share a low. The same split applies to rear speakers. Price the pieces you would still buy before you call the smaller box cheaper.

HDMI eARC, ARC, and optical

The television has to offer the connection the soundbar needs. HDMI eARC and HDMI ARC both send the television's audio back to the bar over an HDMI cable. ARC is the older return path. eARC carries higher-bandwidth audio than ARC, including formats an ARC connection leaves behind. Optical is a separate cable. It carries a smaller set of formats than eARC, and it does not use the HDMI port. A bar that needs eARC falls back to what ARC can carry when the television only has ARC. Both ends have to support the connection you are counting on.

ARC or eARC is often on only one HDMI port, even when the television has several. Read the television's sheet for which port, and look for the label on the set. A bar can also have HDMI inputs of its own. The port marked for the television is the return path. Extra inputs are for a disc player or a console that plugs into the bar. A bar with no extra input means those devices stay on the television. Optical still works on a set with no ARC. A television can omit optical, so check the sheet before you count on it. Whether the television remote will change the bar's volume depends on the connection the sheet names. If that line is missing, you do not know.

A Bluetooth-only bar takes audio from a paired phone. It is a weak match for a television's own apps unless the sheet says the television can send audio to it. Voices that trail the picture are something you will hear once it is connected. Matching the ports on the television, including a sale-season set that drops a port, is in Black Friday TV model numbers.

Derivative model numbers

A model number is the identifier on the carton, on the sheet, and in the cart. A derivative model is a variation on a soundbar or speaker that already exists. On a doorbuster, that variation is often built for the sale, and sometimes sold by one seller. One changed character can drop the subwoofer, drop the rear speakers, swap eARC for ARC, remove an HDMI input, or shorten the warranty. The family name and the photo can still match the one in a review.

The number in the cart is the product you would receive. A review of a similar name describes the model that review named. Write the full number, including any suffix, and match it to a sheet. If you cannot find a sheet for that exact number, pause. A sale-season model can have little price history of its own, and the percent off can point at a list price this version has not sold at. The same mismatch on a television is why a Black Friday TV model number may not match the set in a review. Last year's model under a similar name, on headphones and earbuds, is the parallel check in Black Friday headphone and earbud deals.

The app and multi-room lock-in

A multi-room speaker groups with other speakers that use the same app and the same system. A speaker from a different system will not join that group, even when both use Wi-Fi. Read the system name on the sheet and on the speakers you already own. A bargain that starts a second system is a second set of speakers. Speakers you buy later for the system you already own will not join it. Bluetooth pairs the speaker to a phone. It does not make a multi-room group.

Grouping, a music service, or updates may require an account in that app. Read what still works from the buttons on the speaker if you decline the account. If the page is silent, you do not know. A speaker whose main job is a voice assistant, a microphone, and an account is a different product. What to check on that device is in Black Friday smart home device deals.

Portable Bluetooth speakers

A portable speaker is a different purchase from a soundbar. Read the water rating printed for this model. It is usually a code starting with IP. The second character is water, and a phrase such as "water resistant," with no code, has not named a test. How to read that code is the same check as on a pair you wear, in Black Friday headphone and earbud deals. The code on this speaker is the one you are buying. A port cover left open can take the speaker outside the test the code describes. If the sheet does not say so, treat that as unknown.

The battery hours are the result of a test. Read whether the sheet names the volume that test used. Louder playback uses the battery faster. A claim with no volume is less specific than one that names the test, and your day can be shorter than the number. Stereo on one cabinet can mean two drivers inside that single speaker. Stereo pairing means two speakers, one for the left and one for the right, and the second speaker is another purchase. Read whether pairing is on this model's sheet and whether the second speaker has to be the same model. If the line is missing, one speaker is what the box contains.

A television plus a soundbar

A package that puts a television and a soundbar in one price is two products. Subtract the television-only price when the television is the piece you were buying, or the soundbar-only price when the bar is. What is left is the price of the other piece. Compare that with the same piece sold alone, same model and condition. A piece you would not buy counts as nothing. A soundbar in the package that omits the subwoofer the solo listing includes is a different product, so the comparison stops.

The television in the box can be a different model number from the set you compared, with different ports. That check is in Black Friday TV model numbers. How to split any package, including an extra you would not have bought, is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding?

Peak watts

A peak, maximum, or total watt number on the box is a figure the maker chose. Makers do not share one test, so a higher number does not tell you this bar is louder than another maker's bar. Continuous power, sometimes called RMS, is a different figure from peak when the sheet lists it. Even that number leaves out the drivers, the subwoofer, and the room. Use the watt line to see what this sheet is claiming. Use your room to hear it.

Room size and the wall bracket

A "rooms up to" line is the maker's suggestion for a room they have in mind. A long room, an open plan, and a high ceiling are different rooms. If the sheet names no size, you do not have that suggestion. The return window, below, is how you hear this model in the room you have.

Many bars sit on the stand or mount on the wall under the television. "Wall-mountable" can mean the bar has the holes and the bracket is a separate part. Read the in-the-box list for a bracket, screws, or a template. Add the bracket's price when you will mount the bar and the box does not include it. The television's own stand can also decide whether a bar fits in front of the screen. That fit is part of comparing television model numbers, in Black Friday TV model numbers.

Hear it in your room

The sheet can be right and the bar can still sound wrong where you will use it: boomy, thin, or lost in an open plan. Voices can trail the picture. A portable speaker can be quieter at the volume you actually use. The return window is the listening test. Read the days, who pays return shipping, and whether an opened soundbar or speaker is returnable. Some listings treat opened audio as final sale or charge a restocking fee. The policy on this listing is the one that applies.

When to wait

Wait when the channel count, the in-the-box list, or the connection is missing, when the subwoofer or the rear speakers are sold separately and you have not priced them, when the model number does not match a sheet, or when a multi-room speaker will not join the system you already have. Wait when the discount shows only beside a "was" price. Black Friday vs Cyber Monday and early Black Friday deals cover the calendar. Buy when the cart matches the sheet and checkout is at or under this model's recent low after the subwoofer, the bracket, and the speakers you will still pay for.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the sheet, your television, and this model's prices.

  1. 1What channel count does the sheet list, and is the .1 a subwoofer in this box?
  2. 2Are the surround speakers separate pieces I will place, or virtual processing and upfiring drivers?
  3. 3Does my television have eARC, ARC, or optical on the port this model needs?
  4. 4Is the model number the one in the review, or a derivative that drops the subwoofer, the rears, or a port?
  5. 5Will this speaker join the app and the speakers I already have?
  6. 6On a portable speaker, what water rating is printed, at what volume is the battery claim, and does stereo mean a second speaker?
  7. 7On a television-and-soundbar package, what is left after I subtract the piece I would have bought alone?
  8. 8Is a wall bracket in the box, and can I hear this model in my room before the return window closes?
  9. 9Is checkout at or under this model's recent low for these contents?

When those match, the deal is this soundbar or this speaker.

What do 2.0, 2.1, 3.1, and 5.1 mean on a soundbar?

The first number is the count of main speakers. Two is left and right. Three adds a center. Five adds two surround speakers. The .1 is the low-frequency channel, a subwoofer. A third number, when the sheet lists one, is height channels for sound from above. The count on the box is the claim. The in-the-box list is what arrives, and a surround speaker or a subwoofer may be sold separately.

Is virtual surround the same as speakers placed around the room?

Virtual surround is processing that uses the drivers in the bar to suggest sound from the sides or from behind you. Real surround is separate speakers you place around the room. Upfiring drivers aim at the ceiling so sound reflects down, and they depend on a ceiling that reflects. If the sheet does not say which of these this model uses, you do not know.

Does the soundbar box include a subwoofer?

A .1 in the channel count does not by itself mean a separate subwoofer is in the carton. Some bars produce the low end from drivers in the bar. Some include a separate subwoofer. Some list a subwoofer you buy later. Read the in-the-box list for this model number. Subwoofer ready, or compatible with a subwoofer, is a subwoofer sold separately.

Will HDMI eARC, ARC, or optical work with my TV?

Optical is a separate cable and a smaller set of formats, and it does not use the HDMI port. Can I compare soundbars by the watt number on the box?
