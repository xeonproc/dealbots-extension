Black Friday vs Cyber Monday: which one has the real deals?

Updated October 20, 2026

Neither name means the price is lower. Black Friday and Cyber Monday are two labels on one shopping stretch. The better day is the one where the item you want is cheaper than it has been — and for some items, that is neither day.

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. The dates follow Thanksgiving, so they move next year. The checks below do not. How the two days differ

Black Friday is the Friday after Thanksgiving. It grew up as a day in stores: early openings, a crowd, and a short list of limited-quantity offers advertised as doorbusters. Cyber Monday is the Monday after that weekend. It grew up as a day for buying online, when people were back at a computer instead of standing in an aisle.

The split is softer than the names suggest. Retailers sell online on Friday, and many of the same promotions stay up through Monday. A price can wear a Friday label, a weekend label, and a Monday label without the amount due moving. The names still point at a habit — a store floor on Friday, a checkout page on Monday — but they do not promise a lower price.

Why the better day depends on the item

There is no better day for everything you might buy. A limited-quantity doorbuster may exist only on Friday and be gone by Monday. An online offer may show up on Monday, or it may be Friday's price with a new headline. Two items in the same order can go opposite ways.

"Monday is cheaper" and "Friday is cheaper" are both weak advice. Compare the item, not the calendar. If both days show the same amount due for the same count, size, color, and condition, there is no better day. There is one price with two names.

Inflated "was" prices

A crossed-out "was" price is a reference the seller chose. It does not have to be a price anyone paid, and it does not have to match this count, size, color, and condition. Raise that reference, leave the selling price alone, and both days can advertise a deep cut while the amount you pay stays the ordinary price.

Read the amount due, after any code and before a mail-in rebate you might not receive. If that amount matches what the item has been selling for, the percent off is a label on the regular price. The same trick shows up in ordinary weeks. A holiday headline does not make the reference more honest.

A price that was lower a few weeks ago

Price history replaces the "was" price with prices shoppers were offered. Look back over the last several weeks. About 30 to 90 days is a useful window: 30 days shows a short markup just before the event, and 90 days shows the typical price when the item already goes on sale every month or two. Match the count, size, color, and condition, or the low belongs to a different product.

If today's checkout price is at or under that recent low, it is a real candidate, whichever day the ad names. If the line already hit this number a few weeks ago, Friday or Monday did not create a new deal. It repeated one. No chart? Search the product name plus "price" and read recent posts from deal communities. Keep the dollar amounts, and set aside percentages copied from the product page.

Bundles that hide the per-unit price

A lower package price can be a worse deal. Divide what you will pay by the count, weight, loads, or months of use, and compare that with the single item at its recent price. Skip the bundle's own "was" price.

A "free" extra, a second unit, or a gift card inside the package helps only if you would have bought that extra anyway. Otherwise the bundle still has to beat the single item. A different size, a refill that assumes you already own the hardware, or a subscription that costs more after the first order is a different price. If the price per unit is worse, the day on the label has not fixed it.

Deals that repeat through the whole week

Many offers do not last a single day. A price can go live before Friday, stay up through the weekend, and still be there on Monday under a Cyber Monday heading. Sometimes the page says the promotion runs all week. Sometimes you only notice by comparing the amount due on each day.

If the checkout total is the same before Friday, on Friday, and on Monday, waiting for the named day does not save money. A limited quantity can still sell out, so a price that is actually under the recent low is worth taking when you see it. A repeated price is not more real because the headline changed.

A checklist before you pick a day

Answer these for the exact item, from checkout and from recent prices. If both days have an offer up, run the list twice. The lower honest price wins.

  1. 1What will I pay after codes and shipping, before an unapproved rebate?
  2. 2Is the "was" price a price people paid, or a number the seller printed?
  3. 3Was this dollar amount already the price a few weeks ago?
  4. 4On a bundle, is the per-unit price better than the single item, counting only extras I wanted?
  5. 5Is this offer already running, or set to run, through the whole week under another name?
  6. 6If Friday and Monday differ, which checkout total is lower for this same item?

When the amount due is at or under the recent low, the per-unit price holds up, and the price is not just an earlier price with a new headline, that day has the real deal. If the recent low is unclear, skip the percent off.

Questions people ask

Is Black Friday or Cyber Monday better?

Neither day is better for every item. Compare the checkout price of the exact item with what it has sold for recently. The lower real price is the better day, and sometimes both days show the same price.

When is Black Friday 2026, and when is Cyber Monday?

In 2026, Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. The dates follow Thanksgiving, so they move in other years. The way you check the price stays the same.

Do Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals last only one day?

Often they do not. Many offers run from before Black Friday through Cyber Monday, sometimes with a new headline each day. If the checkout total does not change, it is the same deal.

How do I tell if a Black Friday or Cyber Monday price is actually lower?

Compare the amount you will pay with prices for the same item over the last several weeks. A real deal is at or under that recent low. A price that only beats an inflated was price is the regular price with a new label.

Are bundle deals better on Black Friday or Cyber Monday?

Only if the price per unit is lower than the single item, and only if you wanted every part of the bundle. Divide the package price by the count. A lower package total can still be a worse price per unit. The day on the label does not change that math.
