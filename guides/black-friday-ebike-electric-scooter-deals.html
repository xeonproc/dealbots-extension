Black Friday e-bike and electric scooter deals: what to check before you buy

Updated November 24, 2026

An e-bike or electric scooter deal can look like a deep cut and still be a smaller battery, a range measured with a light rider on flat ground, or a "compare at" price the model has not been selling at. The watt-hours, how the motor behaves, and this model's own prices tell you whether the discount is on the bike or the scooter you will keep.

Watt-hours, not just amp-hours

Watt-hours are the energy the pack stores: volts times amp-hours. A 36 V, 10 Ah pack is 36 × 10 = 360 Wh. A 48 V, 10 Ah pack is 480 Wh. A 36 V, 12 Ah pack is 36 × 12 = 432 Wh, so the higher amp-hour label can be the smaller pack. Sheets that list only amp-hours cannot be ranked. If watt-hours are printed, check that they match volts times amp-hours. Missing voltage and amp-hours means you do not have the capacity.

Why the advertised range is a best case

The miles on the sheet come from a test, often a lighter rider, flat ground, little wind, a mild day, and the lowest assist or a steady low speed. Hills, stops, cargo, a heavier rider, cold, a headwind, and a higher assist use more energy per mile, so the same pack covers fewer miles. If the sheet never describes the test, those miles are a ceiling you cannot place. Your miles equal the watt-hours divided by the watt-hours your own riding uses per mile.

Cell quality and electrical certification

A lithium cell that is poorly made, damaged, or charged past what it can take can overheat and catch fire. Look for a certification of this model's electrical system, battery and charger included. UL 2849 is the test used for an e-bike system. UL 2272 is the test used for many scooters and similar devices. A mark on a different model does not transfer. "Lithium," with no standard, means those faults were not shown as tested on this pack.

The charger, charging, and a replacement pack

Use the charger the sheet names for this pack. A different voltage or a higher current is a different setup from that charger. Follow any line on where to charge, whether to unplug at full, and whether to avoid charging while you sleep. Use a hard surface, off bedding, and not blocking an exit. Stop using a pack that is swollen, cracked, unusually hot, or wet. Price a replacement pack for this model and add it for each one you expect to buy. A pack you cannot buy later makes a worn battery a whole-bike problem.

Hub motor or mid-drive

A hub motor sits in the front or rear wheel and does not use the bike's gears, so a hill is the motor's job at the wheel's current speed. A mid-drive sits at the cranks and drives through the chain and the gears, so you can shift on a climb, and the chain and cassette wear faster. The sheet should say which, and front or rear for a hub. A scooter motor is a hub. Read whether it is one motor or two, and which wheel.

Classes 1, 2, and 3

On many e-bike sheets in the United States, class 1 helps only while you pedal and stops assisting at 20 mph. Class 2 can move the bike with a throttle and stops the motor at 20 mph. Class 3 helps while you pedal and stops assisting at 28 mph. A scooter sheet often prints a top speed instead of a class. The label describes the motor. Where you may ride, on a street, a lane, a path, or a sidewalk, depends on your city and state, and those rules differ. Check them where you will ride. This page does not state them.

Nominal watts and peak watts

Continuous watts, also called nominal, are what the motor is rated to hold. Peak watts are a short burst. 250 W continuous and 500 W peak are different kinds of numbers. Compare continuous with continuous. An unlabeled watt figure, often the one in the headline, may be only the peak.

Rider limit and frame fit

The rider limit is the maker's limit for this frame, these brakes, and this motor. Read whether the number includes cargo. A rack or a scooter deck has its own smaller limit. Frame fit is the standover height or the rider height on the sheet. A step-through frame has a low top tube so you can stand through it. A size letter with no height does not say whether you can stand over the frame.

Tires and suspension

Read the tire size and the pressure the sheet names. On a scooter, a pneumatic tire holds air and can puncture. A solid tire, sometimes called honeycomb, does not go flat and passes more of each bump into the deck and your hands. Suspension is a fork, a rear shock, a stem shock, or none. Travel is how far it moves. Small solid tires and no suspension pass the pavement into the bars.

Brakes, including regenerative

A mechanical disc uses a cable. A hydraulic disc uses fluid, and the sheet has to say so. "Disc" alone does not say which. Regenerative braking slows the wheel with the motor and can put a little energy back into the pack. It fades when the pack is full, and it quits if the motor or the controller fails. Count on the brake that still stops the wheel with the battery disconnected.

Water resistance

An IP code tests the enclosure. The first digit is solids, the second is water, and X means that part was not tested. IPX4 is a splash test. A higher second digit names a different test, such as jets or immersion. Read the code on this sheet, and any line that says not to ride in the wet. A missing rating means water resistance is unknown.

Folded weight, if you have to carry it

If you will carry it up stairs, onto a train, or into a car, read the weight and the folded length, width, and height. A folding claim is not a weight. The latch on the sheet is what holds it folded.

What the checkout leaves out

Add a lock, a helmet, lights, and a shop visit if you will not finish assembly and the brake and wheel check. Oversized shipping can be a large share of the total. Read that price, who lifts the box, and whether it stops at the curb or the door. If it has to arrive by a date, that date is in holiday shipping cutoffs. Brake pads, tires, a controller, a charger, and a pack have to be sold for this model, and a shop near you has to be willing to work on them.

Price per watt-hour, and this model's history

Price per watt-hour is checkout, including shipping and the accessories you still need, divided by watt-hours. Compare similar motors and a class you can ride where you live. A lower sticker with far fewer watt-hours can cost more per watt-hour. Judge checkout against this model's own recent prices for these contents and this condition. A "was," "list," or "compare at" figure is the seller's reference. It may never have sold. How to read the chart is in how to read a price history chart. A reference that never sold is the test in how to tell if a sale is real.

A bike sold with cheap extras

Subtract the bike-only price, same model and condition. What remains is the lock, the helmet, the lights, or the bag. An extra you would not buy counts as nothing, and so does one that does not fit this bike. The rest of the split is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding?

Sending an oversized carton back

Read who pays to ship the oversized carton back, whether a restocking fee applies, and whether assembly or a first ride counts as opened. Put those amounts next to the sale price. Windows and fees are in Black Friday return policies.

Battery, frame, and motor warranty

The battery, the frame, and the motor can be covered for different lengths. The controller and the charger can be separate lines. One headline number is often the frame. Read each length, and who pays to ship a heavy bike or scooter for a claim. A plan at checkout is a second purchase, compared in extended warranties and protection plans. An open-box unit can arrive with a pack that has already been cycled, which is part of refurbished vs new.

When to wait

Wait when watt-hours, the class or top speed, the rider limit, the brake type, or an electrical certification is missing, when the return cost is unknown, or when the cut shows only beside a "was" price. Black Friday vs Cyber Monday and early Black Friday deals cover the calendar. Buy when checkout is at or under this model's recent low after shipping, a replacement pack, and the accessories you will still pay for.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the sheet, the return policy, and this model's prices.

  1. 1What are the watt-hours, and do they equal volts times amp-hours?
  2. 2What test is behind the range, and how do my hills, weight, and assist change the miles?
  3. 3Is this model certified, and is the charger the one named for this pack?
  4. 4What does a replacement pack for this model cost, and can I buy it?
  5. 5Hub or mid-drive, which class or top speed, and are the watts continuous or peak?
  6. 6Does the rider limit cover me and any cargo, and can I stand over the frame?
  7. 7Pneumatic or solid tires, any suspension, and a brake that works with the battery disconnected?
  8. 8What IP rating is printed, and what does it weigh folded?
  9. 9After shipping and the accessories I will actually buy, is checkout at or under this model's recent low?

When those match, the deal is this bike or this scooter.

Questions people ask

How do I compare the battery on an e-bike or an electric scooter?

Compare watt-hours. Watt-hours equal volts times amp-hours. A 36 V pack at 10 Ah is 360 Wh, and a 48 V pack at 10 Ah is 480 Wh. Amp-hours alone skip the voltage, so two packs with the same amp-hour rating can store different amounts. Price per watt-hour is the checkout you will pay, including shipping and the parts you still have to buy, divided by those watt-hours.

Why is the advertised range longer than the miles I will get?

The miles on the sheet come from a test. That test often uses a lighter rider, flat ground, little wind, a mild temperature, and a low assist or a steady low speed. Hills, stops, cargo, cold, a headwind, and a higher assist use more watt-hours per mile, so the same pack covers fewer miles. Where you may ride it depends on the city and the state, and those rules differ. Check the rules where you will ride.

Why does a certification mark on the battery matter?

UL 2849 is a test of an e-bike electrical system, including the battery and the charger. UL 2272 is a test used for many electric scooters and similar devices. A lithium cell that is weak, damaged, or charged past what it can take can overheat and catch fire. A pack with no mark for this model has not shown those faults were tested. A mark on a different model does not transfer.

Should I trust the was price on an e-bike or a scooter?

Compare checkout with recent selling prices for this model, these contents, and this condition. A was, list, or compare-at figure is a reference the seller chose. A bare bike and a bike bundled with a lock or a helmet do not share a history. If that reference never sold, the percent is a label.
