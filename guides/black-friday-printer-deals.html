Black Friday printer deals: what to check before you buy

Updated November 20, 2026

A printer deal can look like a deep cut and still be a cheap body with costly ink, a starter cartridge that runs out quickly, or a "compare at" price the printer has not been selling at. Inkjet, laser, or an ink tank

An inkjet fits occasional color and photos. A laser fits text you print often, and toner does not dry out the way ink can. An ink tank costs more up front and refills from bottles. It pays back when you print enough to use the lower cost per page. An idle inkjet can clog, and a cleaning cycle spends ink. Read the black yield and the color yield separately. A monochrome laser and a color laser are different printers.

Cost per page

Divide the cartridge price by the rated page yield. That yield comes from the maker's test, and your pages can differ. Example only. These are sample numbers. A $40 cartridge rated for 400 pages is $0.10 a page. A $20 cartridge rated for 100 pages is $0.20 a page. Use the cartridge you will buy next. A high-yield cartridge can cost less per page once you divide. Add a drum when the sheet lists one as its own part.

A cheap printer can cost more later

Example only, using those per-page figures. Printer A at $60 and $0.20 a page is $260 after 1,000 pages. Printer B at $150 and $0.10 a page is $250. More pages widen B's lead. Far fewer pages can leave A cheaper. Use the yields on the sheets and your own yearly count. A percent off A does not change the cartridge math.

Starter cartridges

A doorbuster can include starter cartridges with a lower yield than the standard replacement. Example only. A starter rated for 150 pages and a standard cartridge rated for 400 are different supplies. The sale price does not include the next cartridge, and "includes ink" can mean the starters. If the page gives one yield and does not name the cartridge, you do not know which one it is. Installing a cartridge can change the return, covered in Black Friday return policies.

Ink programs, an account, and firmware

An ink program can bill you monthly or ship on a schedule. Read whether you can pause, who owns unused ink, and whether the printer works if you leave. Example only. Five dollars a month is $60 a year. A single $40 cartridge costs less if you print little. Some printers need an account or an app before they print. A firmware update can stop a third-party cartridge that worked on day one. Silence on the sheet means you do not know. A checkout plan is a separate purchase, in extended warranties and protection plans.

Wireless setup and the cloud

Wireless joins your network. An app may be the only setup, and printing away from home may need the maker's service. That feature stops if the service ends or you skip the account. A listed USB or direct connection still prints without it. If none is listed, treat account-free printing as unknown.

Duplex, an ADF, and a scanner

Automatic duplex prints both sides for you. Manual duplex means you flip the stack. An ADF feeds a stack through the scanner. A flatbed scans one page at a time. Pay for duplex when you print multi-page documents often, and for an ADF when you scan stacks. Skip a scanner you will not use, and read how many sheets the feeder holds. A pages-per-minute number comes from the maker's test.

The "was" price and this printer's history

A "was," "list," or "compare at" figure is a reference the seller chose. It may be a price this model has not sold at, or a fuller package than the cart. Compare checkout with recent prices for this model and this condition. A printer and a printer-plus-ink package do not share a low. The chart is in how to read a price history chart. A reference price that never sold is the test in how to tell if a sale is real.

A printer plus extra ink

Subtract the printer-only price from the package. The difference is the ink. Example only. A printer at $80, or $110 with two standard black cartridges, prices that ink at $30. At $18 each alone, you save $6 if you would have bought both. A starter in the pair is a different supply, so the comparison stops. Count a cartridge you would not buy as nothing. When to wait

Wait when the yield, the starter size, or a way to print without an account is missing, when the package treats a starter as a full cartridge, or when the discount shows only beside a "was" price. Black Friday vs Cyber Monday and early Black Friday deals cover the calendar. Buy when checkout is at or under this model's recent low after the supplies you will still pay for.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the sheet, and this printer's prices.

  1. 1Does this inkjet, laser, or ink tank match how much I print?
  2. 2What is the next cartridge's price divided by its rated yield?
  3. 3After a year of supplies, which printer costs less?
  4. 4Are the included cartridges starters, and what does a standard one yield?
  5. 5Do I need an account, an app, or a monthly ink program to keep printing?
  6. 6Can a firmware update block other cartridges, and can I print without an account?
  7. 7Will I use duplex, an ADF, or a scanner, and does this sheet include it?
  8. 8What do the extra cartridges cost once I subtract the printer-only price?
  9. 9Is checkout at or under this model's recent low for the same contents?

When those match, the deal is this printer.

Questions people ask

Is an inkjet, a laser, or an ink-tank printer the one to buy?

An inkjet fits occasional color and photos. A laser fits mostly text at a higher volume. An ink tank fits when you print enough that a lower cost per page pays back the higher printer price. Price a year of supplies for the pages you print before you pick.

How do I compare cost per page?

Divide the cartridge price by the rated page yield on the sheet. Use the cartridge you will buy next. The one in the box can be a starter with a lower yield. A cheaper cartridge can cost more per page when its yield is lower. Your pages can differ from the rated test.

Are the cartridges in the box full-size?

They can be starter cartridges, rated for fewer pages than the standard replacement. Read both yields. If the page lists one yield and does not say which cartridge it describes, you do not know.

Can a firmware update block third-party cartridges?

Some printers accept only the maker's cartridges, and a firmware update can change what the printer accepts after you buy it. Read the sheet and the current terms. If they are silent, you do not know whether another cartridge will keep working.

Is a printer-and-ink bundle cheaper?

Subtract the printer-only price for the same model and condition. What is left is what you pay for the ink in the bundle. Compare that with the same cartridges sold alone. A starter cartridge is a different supply from a standard cartridge.

Should I trust the was price on a printer?

Compare checkout with this printer's recent selling prices for the same contents and condition. A was, list, or compare-at figure is a reference the seller chose. If it never shows up as a selling price, the percent is a label.
