Derivative and seller-exclusive model numbers

A model number is the identifier on the carton, on the spec sheet, and in the cart. A derivative model is a variation on an appliance that already exists. On a doorbuster, that variation is often built for the sale. One changed character can mark a different capacity, a plastic jar instead of a glass one, a different set of included pieces, a different wattage, or a shorter warranty. The family name can still match the appliance in a review.

A seller-exclusive model is that kind of variation sold by one seller. The sheet for the widely sold model does not describe it. A review of the family name describes the model that review named. The number in the cart is the appliance you would receive. The name in the headline is a label.

The mismatch is easy to miss on an air fryer, a blender, a stand mixer, or a coffee maker, because the photo looks like the familiar one and the differences sit in the basket, the jar, the bowl, or the carafe. Write the full model number from the cart, including any suffix. Match it to a spec sheet before you compare a price. If you cannot find a sheet for that exact number, pause. You are being asked to buy an appliance you cannot compare.

A sale-season model can have little price history of its own. The percent off on the page can point at a list price this exact version has rarely sold at, or has not sold at. The appliance can still be a fair buy. It is a different product from the reviewed one until the sheets say otherwise. The same kind of mismatch on a television is why a Black Friday TV model number may not match the set in a review.

Capacity and wattage claims

Open the sheet for the model in the cart. Read the capacity line and the wattage line. A missing line is an unknown. It is not a match for another model in the same family.

Capacity

Capacity is the volume the sheet gives the part that holds the food or the drink. On an air fryer that is the basket or the drawer. On a blender it is the jar. On a stand mixer it is the bowl. On a coffee maker it is the carafe or the reservoir. A "serves" number, a "family size" badge, or a count of people is a portion claim. It is not the volume.

Compare the unit the sheet uses. Quarts, liters, cups, and ounces are different measures. A cup mark on a coffee maker may not be a drinking mug. If the sheet does not say how big that cup is, you do not yet know the volume. Two models can share a family name and list different volumes. The figure on this sheet is the one you are buying.

The amount you can use can be less than the rated volume. A blender jar filled to the rim can overflow once the food moves. An air fryer basket filled to the top can cook unevenly. A mixer bowl rated in quarts still has to turn the dough you actually make. Read the rated volume, then decide whether that volume is the amount you will use.

The form is part of the match. A single basket and a dual-basket air fryer can show a similar total volume and still be different appliances. A full-size blender jar and a personal cup that shares the family name are different products. A tilt-head stand mixer and a bowl-lift stand mixer can share a family name and take different bowls. A drip coffee maker with a carafe and a single-serve machine are different products. Match the form, then match the volume.

Wattage

Wattage is the power the sheet says the appliance draws. On an air fryer or a coffee maker it is the heater. On a blender or a stand mixer it is the motor. A higher number is a higher claimed draw. It is not, by itself, a better appliance. Two models in one family can list different wattages. Compare the numbers on the two sheets, and leave the percent-off badge out of that comparison.

Some blender sheets list watts and also list horsepower. Those are different lines. A horsepower badge does not fill in a missing wattage, and it does not tell you the test behind the figure. If the sheet does not list watts, that line is an unknown. A brew-time claim, a heat-up claim, or a line that says the motor is powerful is a phrase until the sheet gives the watts for this model. Do not copy the figure from a review of a similar name.

The accessory bundle and the base unit

A doorbuster often adds pieces around the appliance: an extra air-fryer rack, a second blender jar, a travel cup, a dough hook, a pasta attachment, a frother, a filter, or a second bowl. The package price is the price of whatever the cart includes. The base unit is the motor or the heater and the one vessel you need to use it. Those are different offers when the extras are what make the percent off look large.

Read the list of what is in the box for this model number. A review of the family may show pieces this box leaves out, or this box may add pieces the base model sells without. Price the base unit on its own, same model and condition, and price only the extras you would buy anyway. An extra you would not use has nothing to add to the saving. A second rack you wanted is part of the purchase. A cup, a frother, or a booklet you would not have bought is an item in the box.

How to add those separate prices, including a printed value that sits above the extra's own selling price, is in Black Friday bundles: real savings or padding? The base unit's own price history is the chart that matters. A chart for the package does not answer for the appliance sold alone, and the appliance's chart does not answer for a different model number that exists only as a package.

Plastic, metal, and parts you can replace

The part that touches food is the part you will live with. A blender jar can be plastic or glass. A mixer bowl can be metal, glass, or plastic. An air fryer basket can be metal with a coating, or another material the sheet names. A coffee carafe can be glass, which can break, or a thermal carafe, which holds heat without a warming plate. The photo can hide the difference. The material line on this sheet is the one to read.

A coating and a bare metal surface wear differently. The sheet may say the basket, the bowl, or the beater is dishwasher-safe, or it may say hand-wash only. If it says neither, you do not yet know how you are supposed to clean it. Two models in one family can use different materials. The line for the number in the cart is the one you are buying.

Then read whether the part that fails is sold on its own for this model number. A blender jar, a jar lid, a blade, a coffee carafe, a carafe lid, an air fryer basket, a mixer bowl, and a beater are the usual ones. A model whose jar or carafe is sold separately can be put back into use by replacing that part. A model whose jar or carafe is not sold separately turns a crack, a clouded plastic jar, or a chipped glass carafe into a replacement of the whole appliance.

Spare parts are listed for a model number. A jar, carafe, basket, or bowl listed for the widely sold model is not automatically a part for the number in the cart. A seller-exclusive model is the one to check twice. If the sheet and the parts list do not name a replacement for this number, treat spares as an unknown.

Noise and the space it takes

Noise

These appliances make sound in the room where you cook. A blender and a stand mixer are motor noise. An air fryer is fan noise. A coffee maker with a grinder adds the grinder. The sheet sometimes lists a decibel figure and sometimes lists none. If it lists one, read the distance and the condition of the test when the sheet includes them. A number without a distance is hard to put next to another number. If the sheet lists no figure, you do not have a measurement. A badge that says quiet has not filled that in.

Two models in one family can sound different because the motor, the housing, and the jar or the bowl differ. A review of the other model does not answer for this one. The return window is how you learn the sound in your kitchen. Read how many days you have and who pays return shipping before you buy. Counter footprint

The footprint is the width and the depth on the counter, plus the height. The height that matters includes the lid open, the mixer head lifted, the blender filled with the lid off, or the coffee reservoir open under a cabinet. The depth that matters includes an air fryer basket pulled out. A unit that fits the product photo can still block a cabinet, a backsplash, or the outlet the cord has to reach.

Read the dimensions on this model's sheet. Measure the space you have, including any clearance the sheet asks for around the unit. A missing dimension is an unknown. Do not take the dimension from a different capacity in the same family. A larger basket, jar, or bowl is often a larger body.

Warranty length across variants

The warranty that comes with the appliance is printed for this model. The length, what counts as a defect, and whether parts and labor are one promise or two can differ between a full-featured model and a sale-season variant with a similar name. A shorter warranty is a smaller offer, even when the price is lower. A length you remember from the reviewed model does not transfer.

Some lines are shorter than the appliance itself. A glass carafe, a jar, a coating, or the motor can be a separate line. Read the warranty page for this model number. If the listing is silent, you do not yet know what you already have.

A protection plan offered at checkout is a second purchase. Compare it with the warranty this variant already includes, not with the warranty on a different model in the family. How to price that plan, including a card benefit that may overlap it, is in extended warranties and protection plans on Black Friday. The plan does not turn a seller-exclusive model into the one you meant to buy. Refurbished, open-box, and renewed units bring their own warranty, which is a different comparison, covered in refurbished vs new on Black Friday.

Check this model's price history

The list price next to "was," "list," or "compare at" is a reference the seller chose. On a doorbuster appliance it may be a figure this exact model has rarely sold at. The percent off uses that reference. The dollar price at checkout is the fact. How a reference price inflates the percent, including a short markup just before a sale, is in how to tell if a sale is real.

A price chart answers for the model on the chart. A low for the widely sold model, for a different capacity in the same family, or for the package with the extras, is a low for that other offer. Search the exact model number in the cart. Match the condition. New, open-box, and refurbished do not share a chart. Read about 30 to 90 days when the chart has them. Thirty days shows a short markup just before the sale. Ninety days is the safer window when that model has already been on sale more than once. A seller-exclusive or sale-season model can have a short chart, or none, because it has not been sold for long. On a short chart, the first advertised price and the sale price can both sit inside the same brief window. Read the dates. If the "was" price only appears for a few days before the sale, or never appears as a selling price, the percent off is a label.

A short checklist

Answer these from the cart, the spec sheet, this model's prices, and the warranty for this variant.

  1. 1Is the full model number the appliance I mean, or a derivative or seller-exclusive variant?
  2. 2What capacity and wattage does this sheet list, in units I can compare?
  3. 3Is the cart the base unit, or a bundle of accessories I would not buy on their own?
  4. 4Are the food-contact parts plastic, glass, or metal, and can I replace the jar, carafe, basket, or bowl for this model number?
  5. 5Will the noise and the footprint, including a lid, a lifted mixer head, or a pulled-out basket, work in the space I have?
  6. 6What warranty length does this variant include, and is it the same as the model I compared it with?
  7. 7Is today's checkout price at or under this model's own recent low, not just under a list price?

When the model number matches what you mean, the capacity and the parts are the ones you will use, and checkout is at or under this model's own recent low, the deal is about this appliance. If the chart belongs to another model in the family, skip the percent off until you have this model's price.

Questions people ask

What is a seller-exclusive model number on a kitchen appliance?

A seller-exclusive model is a variation sold by one seller, often for the sale. The family name can match an appliance people reviewed, and the model number can still mean a different capacity, a different jar or carafe, a different wattage, or a shorter warranty. The number in the cart is the appliance you would receive. A review of a similar name describes that other model.

How should I compare capacity on an air fryer, blender, stand mixer, or coffee maker?

Read the volume on this model's spec sheet: the basket or drawer, the jar, the bowl, or the carafe or reservoir. A serves-several badge is a portion claim, not that volume. Compare quarts, liters, cups, or ounces as the sheet prints them. A cup mark on a coffee maker may not be a drinking mug. If the sheet does not define it, you do not yet know the volume.

Is a bundle of accessories a better deal than the base appliance?

Only when you would buy those extras anyway and the package costs less than the pieces priced separately. The base unit is the motor or heater and the vessel you need to use it. An extra rack, jar, cup, or frother you would not buy is not part of the saving. Price the base unit on its own, at this model number, and use that model's price history.

Why do the materials and replacement parts matter?

The jar, bowl, basket, or carafe is the part that touches food, and it may be plastic, glass, or metal. A coating and a bare metal surface are different to clean and to wear. If a replacement jar, carafe, basket, or bowl is not sold for this model number, a crack or a failed coating means replacing the whole appliance. Spare parts for a similar name may not fit this number.

Can two kitchen appliances in one family have different warranties?

Yes. The length, and whether the motor, a glass carafe, a jar, or a coating is a separate line, are printed for this model. A sale-season variant can carry a shorter warranty than the model you compared it with. A length you remember from the other model does not transfer. A protection plan at checkout does not change the warranty that comes with this variant.

How do I tell if a kitchen appliance discount is real?

Read the price history for this exact model number, not the percent off on the page and not the chart for a similar name or for the bundle. The percent uses a reference price the seller chose. A seller-exclusive model may have a short chart, or none. A checkout price at or under this model's own recent low is the one that is actually lower.
